# The crew of the *Unhurried*

Names, backstories and characters for all 21. Replaces the one-line bios in `sim/roster.js`,
which were job titles with a mannerism attached.

Read `roster-review.md` first for why the old ones failed. The short version: they had no culture
behind them, so they could not be names.

---

## The ship

The survey vessel ***Unhurried*** was commissioned for a four-hundred-year census. The plan was
leisurely on purpose — a ship that would outlive its first crew twice over, drifting between
systems, recording everything alive in a universe understood to be abundant and in no danger
whatsoever.

The plan is now eleven years old and the universe is being erased.

Nobody has renamed the ship. Renaming a commissioned vessel requires a Form 9 countersigned by a
department head who no longer exists, filed with an office that was inside the Null two years ago.
The crew have stopped finding this funny and started finding it restful. *Unhurried* is painted on
the hull in letters four metres high and there is nothing anyone can do about it.

## The Registry

The **Registry of Living Systems** is the institution that owns the ship, and it is a bureaucracy,
not a navy. Nobody aboard has a rank. They have *posts*, and posts come with forms.

Its mission was to catalogue life. Its mission is still to catalogue life. What changed is that the
catalogue is now the only copy — the Null takes a system and leaves nothing behind, no remains, no
ruins, no evidence that anything was ever there. A species that the *Unhurried* does not reach in
time did not merely die. It was never recorded, and so, as far as anything that comes after is
concerned, it never happened.

This is the weight the ship carries and the crew almost never mention it. They argue about
taxonomy instead. A fifteen-year dispute over whether a thing with two mouths counts as one
organism is easier to hold in your head than the other thing, and it is, in its way, the same
argument.

## The Null

Nobody knows what it is. It is not an army, a fleet or a weapon. It arrives, and afterwards the
place it arrived is not damaged — it is *absent*, in a way that makes people who look at it for
too long stop being able to describe what they are looking at.

The Registry's official position is that the Null is a phenomenon under study.

The Registry's official position is also that the *Unhurried* is not fleeing it.

## How names work aboard

Four populations, four conventions. This is the whole naming system, and everything below follows
from it.

**Registry staff** — the scientists. Surname and post, used formally, because they sign things.
*Dr. Adoun, Xenochemistry.* Among themselves they are less formal, but the signature is the
identity.

**Ship's complement** — the people who keep the vessel running. They have names, and nobody uses
them. Within a month of boarding you are given a **trade-name**, earned from an incident, a tool,
or something you said once that you will never live down. Trade-names are affectionate, unflattering
and permanent. Refusing yours makes it stick harder.

**Catalogued entries** — things the survey logged that then declined to leave. They are named by
their entry, or by a fragment of it. The Registry is extremely uncomfortable about this and has
issued three separate memoranda.

**Everyone else** — the manifest has a category called *supernumerary* for people aboard whose
paperwork does not resolve. There are more of these than there should be.

Two non-human conventions matter here:

- The **Cassine** grow their entire lives and never stop. A Cassine takes a new name at each size
  threshold, and each name is longer than the last, so a Cassine's name tells you roughly how old
  and how large they are. It is considered rude to use an earlier one.
- The **Ossuan** have no personal names. They have *posts*, and the post is the name. When an
  Ossuan's function changes, their name changes, and they do not regard the previous holder — who
  was themselves — as the same person in any sense worth arguing about. The Registry has an entire
  subsection of personnel policy about this and it satisfies nobody.

## Hull and protection

### **Gasket** — Hanne Vuorela · hull technician · *order* · (was Wex)

Hanne Vuorela has never in her life allowed a thing to fail before she got to it. She walks the
*Unhurried*'s spine twice a shift with a tablet and a mallet, tapping welds and listening, and she
can tell you which of four thousand seams will go next and roughly when. She is almost always
right.

She is called Gasket because a gasket's entire purpose is to sit in a join and prevent a failure
that would otherwise happen, and because the name annoyed her for about six weeks, which is how
trade-names are chosen.

**Her flaw costs the ship real damage.** She services on schedule. On *the* schedule — the one she
wrote — and she does not accept that a crisis is a reason to defer maintenance. She has taken the
starboard coolant loop offline during an active engagement because it was due, and she will do it
again, and the maddening thing is that the loop would in fact have failed. She is not wrong. She is
just impossible.

She is order in the way the element means it: fixed, predictable, unvarying. Her shield is the same
size every time. It arrives when she said it would. There is no upside case and no bad night. Crew
who have worked with more spectacular protectors find her boring for about two fights and then
never want to fight without her again.

She does not understand why anyone would want a surprise.

### **Antique** — Reva Sokolovna · deck supervisor · *chaos* · (was Tarn)

The boarding plate Reva wears into every fight is Registry catalogue entry 0041, a ceremonial
armour from a culture the survey logged nineteen years ago and which no longer exists. It is
beautiful, it is irreplaceable, it is the last physical object of its kind anywhere, and it is
not very good armour. It was never meant to stop anything. It was meant to be looked at.

Reva takes it out of the specimen hold before every engagement and puts it back afterwards,
usually dented. Ledger has filed eleven formal objections. Reva has read none of them.

She steps in front of things. That is the whole of her tactical philosophy and she is unembarrassed
by it. She is not durable — she is thinner than she looks and the plate does nothing — but a thing
that is hitting Reva is not hitting anyone else, and whatever hits her gets hurt doing it.

**Chaos, precisely as the element means it: all-or-nothing.** She either holds the line completely
or she goes down in one exchange, and which of those happens is not really up to her. She needs
somebody to shield her and she knows it and she goes in anyway.

Ask her why the plate and she will tell you it is the only one left and it should be worn. Ask her
why it should be worn and she will change the subject. Reva is not a mystic and would be offended
by the suggestion. She simply cannot stand the idea of the last of something sitting in a drawer.

### **Ferrule** — Tomás Iriarte · structural welder · *growth*

A ferrule is the metal band around a tool handle that stops it splitting under load, and Tomás
earned the name honestly: he is the reason the ship's worst repairs have held.

He welds during fights. Not metaphorically — he brings the torch, he gets between two plates while
things are happening, and he lays down material. The crew he is nearest get plating thrown over
them whether they asked for it or not, and if you are standing in the wrong place you will be
partially welded to a bulkhead for your own protection. Several people have been.

**Growth: it starts small and gets larger the longer it is not broken.** His first pass is almost
nothing. By the fourth he has built something substantial, and the whole structure is load-bearing
on the assumption that nobody interrupts him. Interrupt him and it is worth nothing at all.

Tomás talks constantly and says very little, which is a deliberate technique — he learned early
that a nervous crew does better work if someone is narrating something boring. He has an
encyclopaedic knowledge of the ship's plumbing and will share it during combat.

His flaw is that he cannot stop improving things. He has modified equipment that was working
perfectly, twice, in ways that were genuinely better and that nobody could subsequently operate.

### **Ballast** — Ojo Mbeki · cargo · *growth*

During the Kessa jettison, with the hold venting and the order given, Ojo Mbeki refused to leave
because there was a live specimen crate in the back and nobody had checked it. He was right. There
was. It is currently in the aft hold, it is enormous now, and it is the reason the survey has a
type specimen for an entire order of life.

He has been called Ballast ever since, and he is completely at peace with it. Ballast is the stuff
you carry that has no purpose except to stop the ship rolling over, and Ojo considers this an
accurate and flattering description of his function.

He is the largest human aboard and the slowest thing in any fight. He arrives late, he absorbs
whatever is going, and he keeps going. Things that hit him feed him. He does not dodge, and when
asked about it he has said, more than once, that dodging is how you end up somewhere you did not
plan to be.

**Growth: he gets more difficult to deal with the longer he is left alone.** Early he is a slow
man. Late he is a problem.

He is aboard because cargo work is cargo work and the pay was the same. He has no feelings about
the mission and finds people who do exhausting. He is, however, the person the younger crew go to,
and he pretends not to have noticed this.

## Blight and contagion

### **Downwind** — Dr. Thessaly Adoun · xenochemistry · *decay* · (was Ash)

There is a standing order aboard the *Unhurried*, unwritten because writing it down would require
naming her, that when Dr. Adoun is working a site you stay upwind.

Thessaly aerosolises. It is her method and it is genuinely the fastest way to characterise an
unknown biosphere — release a reagent, let the atmosphere do the mixing, read what comes back. It
gets results in an hour that careful sampling gets in a week. It also contaminates everything
within a kilometre, permanently, which is how she and Quill came to have the longest-running feud
on the ship.

**Decay, exactly as the element means it: front-loaded and permanent.** What she does happens
immediately and does not wear off. There is no version of a site she has worked that goes back to
how it was.

She is unapologetic and, worse, cheerful. Her position is that the Null is coming and a
contaminated record is infinitely better than no record, and she is right, and being right about
this has made her careless about everything else. She has stopped seeing the difference between
*necessary* and *convenient*.

Thessaly is the most-used member of the crew and the one most people would not choose to have
dinner with. She notices this and has decided it does not matter. She is wrong about that, and it
is the only thing she is wrong about.

### **Halflife** — Nour Bediako · reactor technician · *chaos* · (was Cinder)

Nour has been about to resign for eleven years. They have written the letter. They have shown the
letter to people. The quantity of Nour's intention to leave halves at regular intervals and has
never once reached zero, which is how they got the name and why they have stopped objecting to it.

They work the reactor opposite Halo, and the two of them have made an art of not being in the same
compartment. Halo routes power carefully and gives away exactly what she can spare. Nour does
everything in one irreversible go and then deals with what is left.

**Chaos: all-or-nothing.** What Nour touches keeps burning after they have gone, which is a
property of the reactor work and also a fair summary of their interpersonal life. They commit
totally or not at all, and the not-at-all is most of the time.

The reason they have not left is not the mission, which they consider a filing exercise conducted
during a house fire. It is that Halo would have to do the reactor alone, and Nour has run the
numbers on that, and the numbers are bad. They have never told her this and would deny it under
oath.

Nour is very funny, in a way that makes people uncomfortable about an hour later.

### **Permit** — Dolores Achterberg · coring specialist · *chaos* · (was Pyre)

You cannot survey the inside of something without opening it, and some of the things the
*Unhurried* surveys are the size of moons. Dolores opens them. Her post is coring specialist, which
is the Registry's phrase for a person who detonates things carefully.

She will not detonate anything without written authorisation. Not *should* not — will not. She has
stood at a charge for forty minutes with a hostile organism closing on her position, waiting for a
countersignature, and she got it, and she has never been denied one in her career because nobody on
this ship has the nerve to be the person who refused Permit her form.

Her crewmates find this maddening and rely on it absolutely. A woman who will not blow something up
without permission is a woman who has never once blown up the wrong thing.

**Chaos in the all-or-nothing sense:** she waits, and waits, and lets the pile get large, and then
there is one event and it is over. Her work has no middle. Either nothing is happening or
everything has already happened.

Dolores is warm, chatty, and fond of the younger crew. Her flaw is not the paperwork. It is that
she has developed a taste for the size of the pile, and she now waits slightly longer than the job
requires, for reasons she describes as technical and which are not technical.

### **Sump** — Dr. Abike Sunmonu · field biology · *growth*

Her name is Sunmonu. The first person to get it wrong did so within a day of her boarding, and
Abike — who studies what collects at the bottom of things, who has built a career on drainage,
sediment and the species that live in what everything else discards — decided the mistake was too
appropriate to correct.

She now introduces herself as Sump. The Registry's personnel file has been amended. She thinks this
is the funniest thing that has ever happened to her, and she is nearly alone in that.

Her specialism is transmission: what jumps, when, and to what. She works on the principle that a
contagion is a courier and the interesting question is always the route. When something she has
been studying dies, she watches what it leaves behind and where that goes, and she is usually
already looking at the next host before anyone else has noticed the first one is gone.

**Growth: it heals more the longer it runs, and it spreads on death.** Nothing Abike does is
impressive on the first exchange. By the sixth it is everywhere and cannot be recalled.

She is kind, patient, and slightly frightening to work near, because she is visibly interested in
what is going to happen to you. She has taken samples from crewmates without asking. She does not
see the problem and it has been explained to her four times.

## Drive and tempo

### **Vane** — Aurel Cosma · helm · *energy*

A vane tells you which way the wind is going and nothing else — not how hard, not for how long —
and the crew who named Aurel understood exactly what they were doing.

He reads a fight better than anyone aboard. He can tell you, from the first exchange, how it is
going to go, who is going to be in trouble and where the opening will be. He is right about
direction with a consistency that is genuinely uncanny.

He is frequently wrong about *when*, and he will not concede that these are different skills. Told
that his call was early, Aurel will explain that it was correct, which it was, and the conversation
will end there, and the same thing will happen next week.

**Energy: immediate, and it converts straight into tempo.** What Aurel gives you is not strength,
it is *sooner* — he shoves people into the fight ahead of their turn, and a crew that acts early
is a crew that acts at all. There is nothing over time about him. It lands now or it does not
land.

He came to the *Unhurried* from a courier run, which he will tell you was more exciting and which
he left for reasons he describes as personal. Ballast knows what they are. Ballast has not said.

### **Nettle** — Nettle · supernumerary · *energy*

Nettle's transfer never processed.

They boarded at Hesper Station eleven months ago on a berth that was reassigned in transit, and the
office that would have corrected this was inside the Null before the query arrived. They are on the
manifest under *supernumerary*, they have no post, they draw no salary, and they cannot be put off
the ship because there is no longer any authority competent to receive them.

So Nettle does everything. Badly, immediately, and with enormous enthusiasm. They have assisted in
the reactor, the medical bay, the specimen hold and the galley, all without training, all on the
same afternoon. When anyone aboard begins a task, Nettle appears and joins in.

**This is not a metaphor for their combat role — it is their combat role.** Nettle has no assigned
function, so they follow up on whatever anybody else is doing. A crew of fast, cheap, frequent
actions turns Nettle into the most dangerous thing on the field. A crew of slow deliberate
specialists leaves them standing about.

They are eighteen or nineteen — the records are among the things that did not survive — and the
crew have quietly agreed not to resolve their paperwork, because resolving it would mean sending
them somewhere, and there is nowhere.

Nettle does not know this. Nettle thinks it is an administrative backlog.

### **Mote** — Idris Okonjo · survey drone control · *energy*

The drones are called motes. Idris flies forty of them, from a chair, two decks below whatever is
happening, and after the third year somebody called him by his equipment's name and it stuck.

He hated it. He has explained, with some dignity, that he is the pilot and not the swarm. This has
had the effect you would expect.

Idris is never physically present in a fight, which makes him the only member of the crew who has
never been hit and the only one who watches every engagement from above with full sensor coverage.
He sees everything. He can do very little about most of it. Forty motes can find the crack in a
shell across an entire enemy line in under a second and can apply almost no force to any of them.

**Energy: immediate and wide and gone.** His contribution is entirely in the first instant.

He is the ship's best tactical mind and nobody asks him, because he is two decks down and the habit
never formed. He has stopped offering. Ballast has told him to keep offering. He says he will and
he does not.

## Ordnance

### **Corr** — Beatriz Corriveau · Registry security · *void*

There is one real weapon on the *Unhurried*. It is classified on the manifest as survey equipment,
because declaring it would require the Registry to admit it arms its vessels, which it does not do,
which is why it does, quietly, once.

Beatriz Corriveau is Registry Security. The entire department. She has no colleagues, no deputy and
no successor, and her post exists because some of the things a survey ship meets are enormous and
do not wish to be surveyed.

She has fired the weapon eleven times in nine years. She can tell you about each one, in order,
with dates, and she has never been asked.

**Void: it negates rather than reduces.** What Beatriz shoots does not take damage. It stops being
there. There is no wounded version of what she does and no partial success, and she has thought
about this more than is good for her.

She is the single most powerful individual aboard and she is socially invisible — she eats alone
not from melancholy but because after nine years the habit is set and nobody ever broke it. New
crew routinely do not learn she exists until something goes wrong. Several have assumed the weapon
is automated.

Her one number is very large. She is aware that this is all anyone knows about her and has
developed a dry, unbothered line about it that she deploys at the right moment and that is not
entirely a joke.

### **Otto** — Otaremnivas · specimen handling · *decay* · (was Bosk)

He is Cassine. He is on his fourth name and he will not have a fifth, because the fifth threshold
is one most Cassine do not reach and Otaremnivas has done the arithmetic.

Cassine grow their whole lives and never stop, which sounds like a gift and is a schedule. He is
four metres of patient, unhurried mass, he is the strongest thing on this ship by a factor nobody
has cared to measure, and every year he is slightly larger and slightly slower, and there is a
point past which he will not be able to move himself at all. He knows roughly when that is.

**Decay is his element and he would tell you it is simply accurate: it starts enormous and it
shrinks.** He hits harder than anything else aboard and he hits later, and the arc of him is one
direction only.

The crew call him Otto, which is a shortening of a name that is meant to get longer, and which is
therefore mildly rude, and which he permits because he likes them. He works specimen handling
because he can lift things nobody else can and because he is careful, and the two together are
rarer than either.

He is the best-tempered person aboard. He has one rule, which is that nobody discusses his sizing,
and he has enforced it exactly once, and the crewmate concerned left the ship at the next station.

### **Maul** — Wren Adeyemi · structural analysis · *decay*

A maul is the heavy hammer you use for splitting, and Wren asked for the name. That is unusual and
people noticed.

She was a structural engineer — a good one, on the Hesper orbital ring, on a career track that
would have ended with her name on something enormous. She built for eleven years. Then the Null
took the Kessa systems, and the ring's whole catchment with them, and every structure she had
signed off went from *standing* to *never having been*.

She did not have a breakdown. She filed a transfer. She now works structural analysis for the
survey, which means she looks at a thing and identifies where it will fail, and then she is the
one who puts everything into that point the moment it opens.

**Decay: front-loaded, permanent, no second attempt.** She waits for a break and then commits
absolutely. Nothing Wren hits gets a chance to recover, and she does not carry anything over into
the next exchange, because she has spent it.

She is not bitter and it would be easier if she were. She is *efficient*, in a way that has
hollowed out the parts of the job she used to enjoy. She no longer sketches. Ferrule has noticed,
and has started asking her opinion on repairs she is overqualified for, and she has started giving
it.

## Assay and survey

### **Quill** — Dr. Nadira Quill · xenobotany · *void*

Her actual surname. She is the only member of the crew whose trade-name is simply what she is
called, and the coincidence is so perfect that nobody believes it until they see the file.

Nadira tags every specimen before she touches it. Every one, without exception, including ones that
are visibly about to attack her — she will log a thing while it is coming at her, because an
unlogged specimen is, to her, not yet real, and she will not act on a thing that is not real.

**This is the flaw and it has nearly killed her twice.** It has also produced the most complete
survey record in the Registry's history. Her marks are why half the ship's tactics work at all:
once Nadira has written a thing down, everyone else knows exactly where it is soft.

**Void: it negates.** What she marks does not become weaker. Its protection stops applying. There
is a difference and she will explain it at length.

She has two ongoing wars. The first is with Downwind, who has contaminated eleven of her sites and
considers the objection precious. The second is with Trawl, who logs everything, reads none of it,
and whom Nadira regards as an active vandal — his records are technically complete, technically
compliant and, in her assessment, worthless, and the Registry counts them the same as hers.

That last part is what actually keeps her awake.

---

### **Trawl** — Yusuf Benhaddou · sensors · *void* · (was Vitre)

He paints the whole line at once. Lightly, indiscriminately, in a single sweep, and then he moves
on, and he does not go back and look at what came in.

Yusuf's position — and he has defended it well, in front of people senior to him — is that the Null
is moving faster than the survey and the only honest response is breadth. A shallow record of a
thousand species beats a perfect record of forty. He logs everything. He reads almost none of it.
He believes, sincerely, that somebody later will have time.

Quill thinks he is destroying the thing he is claiming to save. They have not spoken directly in
two years and communicate through the catalogue, in the notes field, at a level of politeness that
frightens onlookers.

**Void, same as Quill, applied at the opposite scale:** he strips protection from the entire line at
once and barely at all. It is broad and shallow and it is exactly as much as he intended.

He is popular. He is funny at dinner. He has never been in a fight he considered personal and finds
the crew's intensity about the mission slightly adolescent. What nobody knows, because he has never
said it and would deny it, is that he checks the Kessa entries. Regularly. They are the only records
he reads twice, and there is nobody left to tell that he was born there.

---

### **Patience** — Dr. Sevim Aydar · cryoanalysis · *order*

Her method takes four hours and cannot be hurried, and she has been called Patience since the
first time somebody asked her to hurry it.

Sevim freezes a shell and waits. She does not crack it. Cooling a structure unevenly puts it under
stress that increases over hours until it fractures along its own weakest line, and the fracture it
chooses is more informative than any hole you could cut. It is the most elegant technique on the
ship. It is also, on a vessel racing an erasure front, almost indefensible.

She defends it anyway, every year, at review, and wins, because her results are not comparable to
anyone else's.

**Order in the truest sense the element has: fixed, predictable, and the same every time.** Sevim's
contribution does not vary by circumstance. She produces the identical result on the first fight of
a voyage and the last.

She is, by some distance, the only person aboard living up to the name painted on the hull, and she
has pointed this out at three consecutive staff meetings.

Her flaw is not slowness — she is correct about slowness. It is that she has quietly stopped
believing the ship will finish the survey, and so she has begun optimising for the quality of the
record rather than its size, and she has not told anyone that this is what she is doing.

---

### **Ledger** — Aurelio Bassi · ship's archivist · *order*

Somebody has to hold the catalogue. Aurelio holds the catalogue.

He is Registry to the bone — third generation, raised in the institution, fluent in its forms in a
way that makes other people's competence look like guessing. He is fast, he is precise, and when
something is entered in the record he acts on it *instantly*, without hesitation, which is his
great strength and the thing that makes him dangerous.

**Because he acts on what is written, not on what is true.** Aurelio has followed a bad entry into
a bad decision and defended it afterwards on the grounds that the entry said so. He was not being
stubborn. He genuinely does not have a mechanism for the record being wrong — it is not scepticism
he lacks, it is the *category*.

**Order: fixed and predictable, following a rule exactly.** Give him a marked target and he answers
it, every time, at the same strength, without deliberation.

He is fifteen years behind on the backlog and it is Quill's fault. She documents at a level of
detail nobody asked for and it all comes to him, and he has never once suggested she do less,
because — and he has said this, once, at the end of a long shift — hers are the entries he would
save if he could only save some.

He has filed eleven objections to Reva removing catalogue entry 0041 from the hold. He will file a
twelfth. He knows exactly how this goes.

## Crew and recovery

### **Bedside** — Dr. Ilinca Marcu · ship's physician · *order* · (was Fen)

She is not a physician. She is a xenobiologist who was, when the last doctor left at Hesper, the
closest thing aboard to one, and the appointment was described as temporary four years ago.

Ilinca is an excellent clinician and a catastrophic one to be treated by. She discusses prognosis in
front of the patient in the register of a field report. She has said the words "structurally, this
is interesting" to a man with a crushed arm. She once described a crewmate's condition, accurately
and within earshot, as *not survivable in the wild*.

The trade-name is entirely sarcastic and she has embraced it with some relish, which has only made
it worse.

**Order: a fixed amount, every turn, reliably.** She does not produce miracles and does not attempt
them. She produces the same competent repair at the same rate, indefinitely, and she has kept more
people alive than anyone aboard by the simple method of never gambling.

She wants her old post back. She says so, often, loudly, in a way that everyone has learned to read
as the opposite. Four years ago she was given a temporary appointment and there has been no serious
attempt to replace her, partly because there is nobody, and partly because she has quietly declined
the two candidates who came close.

### **Navigator** — Navigator · navigation · *growth* · (was Mire)

Ossuan have no personal names. Their post is their name. When the post changes, so does the name,
and the previous holder — who was them — is regarded as a separate person in every way that
matters, which is a position Ossuan hold sincerely and are tired of explaining.

She has been Navigator for forty-one years, which is most of the *Unhurried*'s commissioned life
and longer than anyone else aboard has been anything. She is the only member of the crew who was
present at the commissioning, when the mission was four hundred years long and the name of the ship
was a gentle joke about how much time there was.

Before Navigator, she was something else. She will discuss this in the third person if pressed and
would rather not be pressed.

She is unhurried in the way the ship was supposed to be. Nothing reaches her quickly. She is calm
to a degree that reads as inattention until you have watched her work, and she hits back without
appearing to have noticed that she was hit.

**Growth: the longer it runs, the more she gives.** Navigator is the worst person on this crew to
have in a short fight and the best to have in a long one, and she has an Ossuan's total
indifference to that being an inconvenient shape.

The Registry has a subsection of personnel policy devoted to her filing status. It satisfies
nobody. She finds the whole document very slightly funny.

---

### **Halo** — Ekene Baptiste · reactor engineer · *energy*

The reactor halo is the visible shell of ionisation around a live core, and Ekene spends more hours
inside one than regulation permits, which is where the name came from and why nobody uses it
cheerfully.

Her job is distribution: deciding, moment to moment, who on this ship gets power and who waits. She
is extremely good at it. She is also, structurally, a person whose entire function is giving things
away, and she has never developed a mechanism for stopping.

**Energy: an instant burst and nothing over time.** What Ekene hands you arrives now, in full, and
there is no residue — and when she has given it, she has nothing. She runs cold. Crew who have
worked a long engagement with her have watched her go from the most valuable person on the field to
a woman sitting down against a bulkhead, entirely spent, in the space of two exchanges.

She works the reactor opposite Nour, and the arrangement functions because they are never in the
same compartment. She thinks Nour is reckless. She is right. Nour thinks she will burn herself out
inside five years. Nour is also right, and has told her, once, badly, and it did not land.

She is aboard because the Registry offered her a reactor at twenty-six and nobody else would have.
She has never worked out whether that was a compliment.
