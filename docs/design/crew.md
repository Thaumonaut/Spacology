# The crew of the *Understory*

Names, backstories and characters for all 21. Replaces the one-line bios in `sim/roster.js`.

**Read `world.md` first.** The setting is mistpunk — solarpunk in a temperate rainforest. The ship
is alive, grown from living stone and heartwood. Coolant runs as drinkable rivers with waterfalls.
Navigation means listening to the song left behind by migrating space whales. Magic is infused in
everything, and the six elements are what matter is made of.

Everyone here is a **naturalist** or keeps a living ship running for naturalists. Nobody is a
soldier. The fighting is fieldwork with teeth.

---

## How names work aboard

Four populations, four conventions. Everything below follows from these.

**Census staff** — the naturalists. Surname and discipline, used formally, because they sign
entries into the catalogue and the signature is the identity. *Dr. Adoun, xenochemistry.*

**Ship's complement** — the people who keep a living vessel alive. They have given names and nobody
uses them. Within a month of boarding you are given a **trade-name**, earned from an incident, a
tool or something you said once and will never live down. They are affectionate, unflattering and
permanent. Refusing yours makes it stick harder.

**Catalogued entries** — things the survey logged that then declined to leave. Named by their
entry, or a fragment of it.

**The unplaced** — people aboard whose posting was never agreed. The meeting keeps getting
deferred. There are more of these than there should be.

Two non-human conventions carry two of the crew:

- The **Cassine** grow their entire lives and never stop. A Cassine takes a new name at each size
  threshold and each name is longer than the last, so the name tells you roughly how old and how
  large they are. Using an earlier one is rude.
- The **Ossuan** have no personal names, only **posts**. The post *is* the name. When an Ossuan's
  function changes their name changes, and they do not regard the previous holder — who was
  themselves — as the same person in any sense worth arguing about.

## Hull and protection

### **Poultice** — Hanne Vuorela · stone-tender · *order* · (was Wex)

The *Understory*'s hull is alive and closes its own wounds, given time and encouragement. Hanne is
the encouragement. She walks the ship's spine twice a shift with a tapping-hammer and a kit of
grafts, listening to the stone, and she can tell you which of four thousand growth seams is
thinning and roughly when it will open. She is almost always right.

A poultice is what you pack into a wound to draw it closed. The name annoyed her for about six
weeks, which is how trade-names are chosen.

**Her flaw costs the ship real damage.** She tends on schedule — on *the* schedule, the one she
wrote — and she does not accept that a crisis is a reason to defer it. She has taken a whole
watercourse out of flow during an active engagement because the beds were due for turning, and she
will do it again, and the maddening part is that the beds *were* due. She is not wrong. She is
impossible.

She is order exactly as the element means it: fixed, predictable, unvarying. Her shield is the same
size every time and arrives when she said it would. No good nights, no bad ones. Crew who have
worked with more spectacular protectors find her boring for about two fights and then never want to
work without her.

She does not understand why anyone would want a surprise.

### **Antique** — Reva Sokolovna · deck supervisor · *chaos* · (was Tarn)

What Reva wears into every engagement is catalogue entry 0041: the display carapace of a reef
animal the survey logged nineteen years ago, from a reef that is no longer there. It is
iridescent, it is the last one anywhere, and it is not armour. It was grown to be *looked at*. It
was never meant to stop anything.

She takes it out of the vault before every fight and puts it back afterwards, usually cracked
somewhere new. Ledger has filed eleven formal objections. Reva has read none of them.

She steps in front of things. That is the entirety of her tactical philosophy and she is
unembarrassed by it. She is not durable — she is thinner than she looks and the shell does nothing
— but a thing hitting Reva is not hitting anybody else, and whatever hits her gets hurt doing it.

**Chaos, precisely as the element means it: all-or-nothing.** She holds the line completely or she
goes down in a single exchange, and which one is not really up to her. She needs somebody to shield
her, she knows it, and she goes in anyway.

Ask why the shell and she will say it is the only one left and it should be worn. Ask why it should
be worn and she will change the subject. Reva is not a mystic and would be offended by the
suggestion. She cannot stand the idea of the last of something sitting in a drawer.

### **Cambium** — Tomás Iriarte · grafter · *growth* · (was Ferrule)

The cambium is the thin living layer under bark that does all the actual growing — the part that
closes a wound and lays down new wood. Tomás is the ship's, and he is the reason her worst injuries
have held.

He grafts during fights. Not metaphorically: he brings the kit, he gets in between two failing
members while things are happening, and he splices living wood into the gap. Whoever is nearest
gets bark thrown over them whether they asked or not, and if you are standing in the wrong place
you will be partially grown into a bulkhead for your own protection. Several people have been.

**Growth: it starts at almost nothing and gets larger the longer it is left unbroken.** His first
pass is a smear of sap. By the fourth he has built something structural, and all of it assumes
nobody interrupts him. Interrupt him and it was worth nothing.

Tomás talks constantly and says very little, which is deliberate — he learned early that a nervous
crew does better work with someone narrating something boring. He has an encyclopaedic knowledge of
the ship's watercourses and will share it mid-engagement.

His flaw is that he cannot stop improving things. He has twice modified equipment that was working
perfectly, in ways that were genuinely better and that nobody else could subsequently operate.

### **Ballast** — Ojo Mbeki · hold-keeper · *growth*

During the Kessa venting, with the grove decompressing and the order given, Ojo refused to leave
because there was a live seedling crate at the back and nobody had checked it. He was right. There
was. It is in the aft grove now, it is eleven metres tall, and it is the only type specimen for an
entire order of life.

He has been Ballast ever since and is completely at peace with it. Ballast is the weight you carry
for no purpose except to stop the ship rolling, and Ojo considers that an accurate and flattering
description of his function.

He is the largest human aboard and the slowest thing in any fight. He arrives late, absorbs
whatever is going, and keeps going. Things that hit him feed him. He does not dodge, and asked
about it has said more than once that dodging is how you end up somewhere you did not plan to be.

**Growth: he gets harder to deal with the longer he is left alone.** Early, he is a slow man.
Late, he is a problem.

He took the post because hold work is hold work and the berth was warm. He has no feelings about
the mission and finds people who do exhausting. He is also the person the younger crew go to, and
he pretends not to have noticed.

## Blight and contagion

### **Downwind** — Dr. Thessaly Adoun · xenochemistry · *decay* · (was Ash)

There is a standing rule aboard the *Understory*, never formally agreed because agreeing it would
mean naming her, that when Dr. Adoun is working a reef you stay upwind.

Thessaly releases reagent mist and reads what comes back. It is genuinely the fastest way to
characterise an unknown interior — let the atmosphere do the mixing, sample the response — and it
gets in an hour what careful work gets in a week. It also permanently alters everything within a
kilometre, which is how she and Quill came to have the longest-running feud aboard.

**Decay, exactly as the element means it: front-loaded and permanent.** What she does happens at
once and does not wear off. No reef she has worked goes back to what it was.

She is unapologetic and, worse, cheerful. Her position is that the Null is coming and a
contaminated record beats no record, and she is right, and being right about this has made her
careless about everything else. She has stopped seeing the difference between *necessary* and
*convenient*.

Thessaly is the most-used member of the crew and the one most people would not choose to eat with.
She has noticed and decided it does not matter. She is wrong about that, and it is the only thing
she is wrong about.

### **Duff** — Nour Bediako · heartwood-tender · *chaos* · (was Cinder)

Duff is the deep layer of half-rotted needles on a forest floor, and its notable property is that a
fire can go into it, disappear, and keep burning underground for months after everyone has agreed
the fire is out.

Nour has been about to leave for eleven years. They have written the letter. They have shown people
the letter. The quantity has halved at regular intervals and has never reached zero, and the name
covers both that and the way their work keeps smouldering long after they have walked away.

They tend the heartwood opposite Halo and the arrangement functions because the two of them are
never in the same chamber. Halo gives away exactly what she can spare, carefully. Nour does
everything in one irreversible go and deals with what is left.

**Chaos: all-or-nothing.** They commit totally or not at all, and not-at-all is most of the time.

The reason they have not left is not the mission, which they regard as a filing exercise conducted
during a housefire. It is that Halo would have to tend the heartwood alone, and Nour has run the
numbers on that, and the numbers are bad. They have never told her and would deny it under oath.

Nour is very funny in a way that makes people uncomfortable about an hour later.

### **Permit** — Dolores Achterberg · reef-opener · *chaos* · (was Pyre)

You cannot survey the inside of a reef without opening it, and some reefs are the size of moons.
Dolores opens them. Her post is reef-opener, which is the Census's phrase for a person who
detonates things very carefully.

She will not open anything without agreed consent. Not *should* not — will not. She has stood at a
set charge for forty minutes with something large coming up the gallery behind her, waiting on a
consensus she had every reason to expect, and she got it, and she has never once been refused,
because nobody on this ship has the nerve to be the person who told Permit no.

Her crewmates find this maddening and rely on it absolutely. A woman who will not open a reef
without agreement is a woman who has never once opened the wrong reef.

**Chaos in the all-or-nothing sense.** She waits, and waits, and lets the pile get large, and then
there is one event and it is finished. Her work has no middle. Either nothing is happening or
everything already has.

Dolores is warm, chatty and fond of the younger crew. Her flaw is not the consensus. It is that she
has developed a taste for the size of the pile, and now waits slightly longer than the job needs,
for reasons she describes as technical and which are not technical.

### **Sump** — Dr. Abike Sunmonu · rot ecology · *growth*

Her name is Sunmonu. The first person to get it wrong did so within a day of her boarding, and
Abike — who studies seeps, rot-pools and everything that lives in what the rest of the forest
discards — decided the mistake was too appropriate to correct. She now introduces herself as Sump.
The personnel record has been amended. She thinks it is the funniest thing that has ever happened
to her and she is nearly alone in that.

Rot ecology is a senior discipline aboard, not a lesser one. In a rainforest, decay is the engine:
a fallen trunk becomes a nurse log and the next century stands on top of it. Abike's specialism is
*transmission* — what jumps, when, to what. She works on the principle that a contagion is a
courier and the interesting question is always the route.

**Growth: it gives more the longer it runs, and it spreads on death.** Nothing she does is
impressive in the first exchange. By the sixth it is everywhere and cannot be recalled.

She is kind, patient, and slightly frightening to stand near, because she is visibly interested in
what is going to happen to you. She has taken samples from crewmates without asking. It has been
explained to her four times.

She takes the Null more personally than anyone aboard, and says so less. Rot that gives nothing
back is, to her, not merely a threat. It is an obscenity.

## Drive and tempo

### **Vane** — Aurel Cosma · helm · *energy*

A vane tells you which way the wind is going and nothing else — not how hard, not for how long —
and the crew who named Aurel knew exactly what they were doing.

He reads a fight better than anyone aboard. From the first exchange he can tell you how it will go,
who is about to be in trouble, and where the opening will be. His read on *direction* is uncanny.

He is frequently wrong about *when*, and will not concede these are different skills. Told his call
was early, Aurel explains that it was correct — which it was — and the conversation ends there, and
the same thing happens the following week.

**Energy: immediate, and it converts straight into tempo.** What Aurel gives is not strength, it is
*sooner*. He shoves people into a fight ahead of their turn, and a crew that acts early is a crew
that acts at all. Nothing about him is over time. It lands now or not at all.

He came to the *Understory* off a courier run along the outer lanes, which he will tell you was
more exciting and which he left for reasons he calls personal. Ballast knows what they are. Ballast
has not said.

### **Nettle** — Nettle · unplaced · *energy*

Nettle's posting was never agreed.

They came aboard at a reef station eleven months ago on a berth that was reassigned in transit, and
the placement meeting has been deferred four times — twice for genuinely good reasons and twice
because the agenda ran long. They hold no post, draw no allocation, and cannot be put off the ship
because there is nowhere within reach to put them.

So Nettle does everything. Badly, immediately, enthusiastically. They have assisted at the
heartwood, in the medical hollow, in the specimen grove and in the galley, all untrained, all on
the same afternoon. When anybody aboard begins a task, Nettle materialises and joins in.

**This is not a metaphor for their combat role. It is their combat role.** Having no assigned
function, they follow up on whatever anyone else is doing. A crew of fast, cheap, frequent actions
makes Nettle the most dangerous thing on the field. A crew of slow deliberate specialists leaves
them standing about.

They are eighteen or nineteen; the records were on a station that is no longer anywhere. The crew
have quietly agreed to keep deferring the meeting, because resolving it means sending them
somewhere, and there is nowhere.

Nettle does not know this. Nettle thinks it is a scheduling problem.

### **Mote** — Idris Okonjo · mote-handler · *energy*

The motes are alive — forty drifting scouts the size of a thumb, grown for the purpose, something
between a seed and a jellyfish. Idris handles all forty at once, from a hollow two decks from
wherever the trouble is, and after the third year somebody called him by his swarm's name and it
took.

He hated it. He has explained, with dignity, that he is the handler and not the swarm. This has had
the effect you would expect.

He is never physically present in a fight, which makes him the only crewmate who has never been hit
and the only one who watches every engagement from above with the whole reef laid out. He sees
everything. He can do very little about most of it — forty motes can find the soft seam on an
entire enemy line in under a second and can apply almost no force to any of it.

**Energy: immediate, wide, and gone.** His whole contribution is in the first instant.

He is the best tactical mind aboard and nobody asks him, because he is two decks down and the habit
never formed. He has stopped offering. Ballast has told him to keep offering. He says he will, and
does not.

## Ordnance

### **Corr** — Beatriz Corriveau · deterrent · *void*

Here is the uncomfortable thing about Beatriz, and everyone aboard has felt it and nobody has said
it to her face: **what she does is a small, deliberate, well-aimed version of what the Null does.**

Her post is deterrent — the single post of its kind, created because some reef life is enormous and
does not wish to be catalogued. What she carries does not wound. There is no damaged version of
what Beatriz does and no partial success. A thing she uses it on stops being there, and afterwards
there is nothing to record.

On a ship of naturalists, in a culture built on the belief that nothing is ever really lost, that
makes her quietly radioactive. She is not disliked. She is *avoided*, in a way nobody would admit
to and everybody participates in.

**Void: it negates rather than reduces.** She has thought about this more than is good for her.

She has used it eleven times in nine years. She can tell you about each one, in order, with dates,
and has never been asked.

She is the single most powerful individual aboard and she eats alone, and new crew routinely do not
learn she exists until something goes badly. Several have assumed the post is automated. She has a
dry line about it that she deploys at the right moment and that is not entirely a joke.

### **Otto** — Otaremnivas · specimen handling · *decay* · (was Bosk)

He is Cassine. He is on his fourth name and there will not be a fifth, because the fifth threshold
is one most Cassine do not reach and Otaremnivas has done the arithmetic.

Cassine grow their whole lives and never stop, which sounds like a gift and is a schedule. He is
four metres of patient unhurried mass, the strongest thing aboard by a margin nobody has bothered
to measure, and every year he is a little larger and a little slower, and there is a point past
which he will not be able to move himself at all. He knows roughly when that is.

**Decay is his element and he would tell you it is simply accurate: it starts enormous and it
shrinks.** He hits harder than anything else on the ship and he hits late, and the arc runs one
direction only.

The crew call him Otto, a shortening of a name that is supposed to get longer, which is therefore
mildly rude, and which he permits because he likes them. He handles specimens because he can lift
what nobody else can and because he is careful, and the two together are rarer than either.

He is the best-tempered person aboard. He has one rule — nobody discusses his sizing — and he has
enforced it exactly once, and the crewmate concerned left at the next station.

### **Maul** — Wren Adeyemi · structural analysis · *decay*

A maul is the heavy wedge you split with, and Wren asked for the name. That is unusual and people
noticed.

She was a grower. A good one — she raised living structures on the Kessa reef rings for eleven
years, the slow craft of coaxing stone and heartwood into shapes that hold, and she was on a track
that ended with something enormous carrying her mark. Then the Null took Kessa, and every structure
she had raised went from *standing* to *never having been*.

She did not break down. She filed a transfer. She now does structural analysis, which means she
looks at a thing and finds where it will fail, and then she is the one who puts everything into
that point the instant it opens.

**Decay: front-loaded, permanent, no second attempt.** She waits for a break and commits
absolutely. Nothing she hits recovers, and she carries nothing into the next exchange, because it
is spent.

She is not bitter and it would be easier if she were. She is *efficient*, in a way that has
hollowed out the parts of the work she used to love. She has not grown anything in four years.
Cambium noticed, and started asking her opinion on grafts she is wildly overqualified for, and she
has started giving it.

## Assay and survey

### **Quill** — Dr. Nadira Quill · xenobotany · *void*

Her actual surname. She is the only crewmate whose trade-name is simply what she is called, and the
coincidence is so neat that nobody believes it until they see the record.

Nadira logs every specimen before she touches it. Every one, without exception, including ones
visibly in the act of coming at her — she will complete an entry while a thing is closing, because
an unlogged specimen is, to her, not yet real, and she does not act on things that are not real.

**This is the flaw and it has nearly killed her twice.** It has also produced the most complete
survey record in the Census's history. Her marks are why half the ship's tactics function at all:
once Nadira has written a thing down, everyone knows exactly where it is soft.

**Void: it negates.** What she marks does not become weaker — its protection stops applying. There
is a difference and she will explain it at length.

She has two wars. The first is with Downwind, who has altered eleven of her reefs and treats the
objection as precious. The second is with Trawl, who logs everything, reads none of it, and whom
Nadira regards as an active vandal — his entries are complete, compliant and, in her assessment,
worthless, and the catalogue counts them exactly the same as hers.

That last part is what actually keeps her awake.

### **Trawl** — Yusuf Benhaddou · reef survey · *void* · (was Vitre)

He sweeps the whole reef at once. Lightly, indiscriminately, in a single pass, and then moves on
and does not go back to read what came in.

Yusuf's position — and he has argued it well, in front of people senior to him — is that the Null
is moving faster than the Census and the only honest answer is breadth. A shallow record of a
thousand species beats a perfect record of forty. He logs everything. He reads almost none of it.
He sincerely believes somebody later will have time.

Quill thinks he is destroying the thing he claims to be saving. They have not spoken directly in
two years and communicate through the catalogue, in the annotation field, at a level of politeness
that frightens onlookers.

**Void, the same as Quill, at the opposite scale:** he strips protection from the entire line at
once and barely at all. Broad, shallow, and exactly as much as he intended.

He is popular. He is funny at dinner. He has never been in a fight he took personally and finds the
crew's intensity about the mission faintly adolescent. What nobody knows, because he has never said
it and would deny it, is that he rereads the Kessa entries. Regularly. They are the only records he
reads twice, and there is nobody left to tell that he grew up there.

---

### **Patience** — Dr. Sevim Aydar · mist assay · *order* · (was Rime)

Her method takes four hours and cannot be hurried, and she has been Patience since the first time
somebody asked her to hurry it.

Sevim works with the ship's own cold fog. She walks it over a shell and waits. She does not cut,
crack or force anything — cooling a structure unevenly puts it under a stress that climbs for hours
until it opens along its own weakest grain, and the seam it chooses tells you more than any hole
you could have made. It is the most elegant technique aboard. On a ship racing an erasure front it
is also almost indefensible.

She defends it anyway, every year, at review, and wins, because her results are not comparable to
anybody else's.

**Order in the truest sense the element has: fixed, predictable, identical every time.** Sevim's
contribution does not vary with circumstance. She produces the same result on the first reef of a
voyage and the last.

She is by some distance the only person aboard actually living up to the name on the hull, and she
has pointed this out at three consecutive gatherings.

Her flaw is not slowness — she is correct about slowness. It is that she has quietly stopped
believing the Census will finish, and has begun optimising for the *quality* of the record rather
than its size, and has told nobody that this is what she is doing.

### **Ledger** — Aurelio Bassi · keeper of the catalogue · *order*

The catalogue is the largest single organism aboard after the ship — a grown archive, a coral of
record, every entry laid down in living matter. Somebody has to keep it. Aurelio keeps it.

He is Census to the bone, third generation, raised inside the project, fluent in its practice in a
way that makes other people's competence look like guessing. He is fast and precise, and when
something is in the record he acts on it *instantly*, without hesitation, which is his great
strength and the thing that makes him dangerous.

**Because he acts on what is written, not on what is true.** Aurelio has followed a bad entry into
a bad decision and defended it afterwards on the grounds that the entry said so. He was not being
stubborn. He genuinely has no mechanism for the record being wrong — it is not scepticism he lacks,
it is the *category*.

**Order: fixed and predictable, a rule followed exactly.** Give him a marked target and he answers
it, every time, at the same strength, without deliberation.

He is fifteen years behind on the backlog and it is Quill's fault. She documents at a depth nobody
asked for and all of it comes to him, and he has never once suggested she do less, because — and he
said this once, at the end of a long shift — hers are the entries he would save if he could only
save some.

He has filed eleven objections to Reva removing entry 0041 from the vault. He will file a twelfth.
He knows exactly how this goes.

## Crew and recovery

### **Bedside** — Dr. Ilinca Marcu · ship's physician · *order* · (was Fen)

She is not a physician. She is a xenobiologist who was, when the last doctor left, the closest thing
aboard to one, and the appointment was described as temporary four years ago.

Ilinca is an excellent clinician and a catastrophic one to be treated by. She discusses prognosis in
front of the patient in the register of a field entry. She has said the words "structurally, this
is interesting" to a man with a crushed arm. She once described a crewmate's condition, accurately
and within earshot, as *not survivable in the wild*.

The trade-name is entirely sarcastic and she has embraced it with relish, which has made it worse.

**Order: a fixed amount, every turn, reliably.** She does not produce miracles and does not attempt
them. She produces the same competent repair at the same rate indefinitely, and has kept more
people alive than anyone aboard by the simple method of never gambling.

She wants her old discipline back. She says so often and loudly, in a way everyone has learned to
read as the opposite. There has been no serious attempt to replace her, partly because there is
nobody, and partly because she has quietly talked the two near-candidates out of it.

### **Navigator** — Navigator · navigation · *growth* · (was Mire)

Ossuan have no personal names. The post is the name. When the post changes so does the name, and
the previous holder — who was them — is a separate person in every way that matters, a position
Ossuan hold sincerely and are tired of explaining.

She has been Navigator for forty-one years, which is most of the *Understory*'s life and longer
than anyone else aboard has been anything. She was there at the growing, when the Census was four
hundred years long and the ship's name was a gentle joke about how much time there was.

**Navigation means listening.** The lanes are whale song — routes the great migrations have sung
into the deep for longer than anyone has been recording — and a navigator is someone who can hold a
song in her head for four decades and hear the day it thins. She has lost eleven lanes in eleven
years. She can name all of them. She can still sing four.

Before Navigator, she was something else. She will discuss this in the third person if pressed and
would rather not be pressed.

Nothing reaches her quickly. She is calm to a degree that reads as inattention until you have
watched her work, and she hits back without appearing to have noticed she was hit.

**Growth: the longer it runs, the more she gives.** She is the worst person on this crew to have in
a short fight and the best in a long one, and has an Ossuan's total indifference to that being an
inconvenient shape.

---

### **Halo** — Ekene Baptiste · heartwood-tender · *energy*

The heartwood blooms when it is working — a slow shell of light in the deep chamber, and Ekene
spends more hours inside it than anyone thinks is wise. That is where the name came from and it is
not used cheerfully.

Her work is distribution: deciding, moment to moment, who on this ship gets what the heartwood is
giving and who waits. She is extremely good at it. She is also, structurally, a person whose entire
function is handing things to other people, and she has never developed a mechanism for stopping.

**Energy: an instant burst and nothing over time.** What Ekene gives arrives now, in full, with no
residue — and once given, she has nothing. She runs cold. Crew who have worked a long engagement
with her have watched her go from the most valuable person on the field to a woman sitting down
against a root buttress, entirely spent, in the space of two exchanges.

She tends opposite Nour and the arrangement works because they are never in the same chamber. She
thinks Nour is reckless. She is right. Nour thinks she will burn herself out inside five years.
Nour is also right, and said so once, badly, and it did not land.

She is aboard because the Census offered her a heartwood at twenty-six and nobody else would have.
She has never worked out whether that was a compliment.

legible runs between them.
