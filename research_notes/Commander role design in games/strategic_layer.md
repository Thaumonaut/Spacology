# Strategic-layer commanding role in single-player strategy games

Scope note: these notes come from about 19 searches and fetches done on 2026-10-12. Most of the evidence comes from reviews, wikis, guides and forums from 2012 to 2024. Primary developer statements were scarce: GDC Vault talks were paywalled or announcement-only, and the USgamer Solomon interview returned HTTP 503. Facts dated before 2020 are flagged as older. Some games (Plague Inc, Civilization, Darkest Dungeon, Battle Brothers) got no searches that returned citable material, so they appear only under Gaps.

## Per-game account (loop, clock, intel, trade-offs, tactical link, pacing, sentiment)

### Takeaway
The best-documented designs (XCOM EU/EW, XCOM 2, Into the Breach, Pandemic) share three features. There is one readable escalating pressure, a forced choice of "one of N" that leaves visible neglect elsewhere, and intel that is gathered as part of the pressure rather than as a separate chore. The criticised designs (Phoenix Point at launch, Terra Invicta, Highfleet, HoI4 battle plans) fail on opacity, repetition or unreliable automation.

### Cited Findings

**XCOM: Enemy Unknown / Enemy Within (2012/2013, older)**
- Loop: satellites reduce a country's 5-level panic, drive funding, and are graded at the monthly Council report. A country in full panic leaves at the end-of-month report. — [Prima Games starter guide](https://primagames.com/news/xcom-enemy-unknown-starter-guide)
- Forced choice: "A few times each month, you'll get a report of up to three separate attacks happening simultaneously". You pick one, and the ignored countries' panic rises. — [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/news/xcom-enemy-unknown-starter-guide); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=35142)
- One abduction can take a country from 3/5 to 5/5 panic. In Enemy Within, ignoring or failing an abduction can cause immediate withdrawal (this is guide-reported and the numbers vary between sources). — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/200510/discussions/0/846965056706173067); [TrueAchievements EW basics](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/XCOM-Enemy-Within/walkthrough/2)
- Continent bonuses reward concentration. Guides advise covering two continents first. Expert players deliberately sacrifice low-value regions ("if the big money contributors are safe, then I don't worry so much about Africa"). — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/200510/discussions/0/611702631229587679); [Paizo thread](https://paizo.com/threads/rzs2oypt)
- Criticism: players learn to hold satellites until just before the Council report, which makes the monthly cycle exploitable and predictable. Players also disagree on how mechanics work, which signals opacity. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/200510/discussions/0/611702631229587679)

**XCOM 2 (2016, older) Geoscape**
- Loop: the Avenger is a mobile base. "Instead of waiting for blips on the geoscape, players fly to new continents and make contact with pockets of resistance." Producer Garth DeAngelis: "You're not just standing and waiting for a mission… You're now moving the Avenger around proactively"; "it was sort of a bold move." — [PCGamesN, XCOM 2 Declassified](https://pcgamesn.com/xcom-2/xcom-2-declassified-firaxis-on-the-risky-shot-theyre-taking-with-a-new-strategy-layer)
- Scanning: time is frozen on the Geoscape until you scan. Parking over a site spends days, and an alien attack or mission can interrupt a scan. The monthly supply drop must itself be scanned to collect. — [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/xcom-2-review); [XCOM Wiki: Geoscape (XCOM 2)](https://xcom.fandom.com/wiki/Geoscape_(XCOM_2)); [XCOM Wiki: Supplies](https://xcom.fandom.com/wiki/Supplies)
- PC Gamer: the game uses "scarcity of opportunity" to force dilemmas, with only about six scan sites available at a time. — [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/xcom-2-review)
- Region contact uses Intel, which is scarcer than Supplies. More contacted regions and radio relays increase the supply drop, and Dark Events (for example Rural Checkpoints) shrink it. — [XCOM Wiki: Supplies](https://xcom.fandom.com/wiki/Supplies)
- Dark Events: DeAngelis says players "can't mitigate all of them" and "You don't know exactly what the aliens are going to be doing". He also said: "We want there to be spikes of difficulty… peaks and valleys as opposed to this linear curve." — [PCGamesN](https://pcgamesn.com/xcom-2/xcom-2-declassified-firaxis-on-the-risky-shot-theyre-taking-with-a-new-strategy-layer)
- Avatar clock: the visible 12-pip counter is reduced by story missions and facility raids. Unblocked Dark Events add one or two pips. When it fills, a roughly 20-day countdown starts. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/268500/discussions/0/412446890549174019); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/268500/discussions/0/135507780428639842)
- Sentiment for: "You'll have to play fast and smart and take some risks… some of the game's best and most tense moments". The clock is "thematically on point" and more dynamic than EU's panic. — [Destructoid review](https://destructoid.com/?p=179947); [TheSixthAxis review](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2016/02/12/xcom-2-review/)
- Sentiment against: the clock "functions adequately for the first 50%" and then becomes easy to control (one critic kept it at 3–4 of 12). Players can stall it "for months" by raiding facilities. One reviewer notes the feature "received criticism from some players" while defending it. — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/thoughts-xcom-2/); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/268500/discussions/0/1696040635907895337)
- Developer intent on the Avatar clock: I found no primary Jake Solomon quote. A fan-guide site claims he pushed the Avatar Project as a global doom clock and paired it with mission timers to stop slow, defensive play, but it cites no primary source. Treat it as unverified. — [shapes.inc fan guide](https://shapes.inc/fandom/xcom-2/author)

**Phoenix Point (2019, older; launch-era reviews)**
- Loop: scan around your bases to reveal landmarks and send squads to them. Each landmark extends scanning, so your reach expands slowly. — [Dread Central review](https://www.dreadcentral.com/reviews/474381/phoenix-point-review-two-steps-forward-one-step-back/)
- Criticism: the Geoscape is "mostly ruined by the unpolished dynamics of the three factions". Points of interest were unresponsive, and the game was buggy at launch. Aircraft ETA isn't shown, so you guess whether a defence arrives in time. The Geoscape doesn't always pause on important events. — [Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/pc/phoenix-point); [GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/?p=42747); [Turn Based Lovers Year One review](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/phoenix-point-year-one-review)
- Praise: the strategic layer gives "a sense of risk/reward". — [Film Stories](https://filmstories.co.uk/?p=82964)

**Pandemic (board game, 2008, older)**
- Matt Leacock was "interested in emergent systems and found the outbreaks and chain reactions fascinating to model". — [Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2014/10/13/yahtzee-meets-the-hot-zone/)
- Epidemic cards add a new outbreak and reshuffle the infection discard pile onto the top of the deck, so already-hit cities return. Leacock "stumbled on [it] by accident". It produces "escalating moments of hope and fear". Difficulty is tuned by the number of Epidemic cards. — [Nerdist](https://nerdist.com/article/10-years-of-saving-the-world-a-look-at-the-pandemic-family-of-games/)
- Leacock moved away from an educational, fact-heavy design and focused on emotions. — [Scienza in rete](https://www.scienzainrete.it/node/18119?page=1)

**FTL (2012, older)**
- Pressure: the rebel fleet pursues you across 8 sectors of about 20 beacons. Each jump uses fuel and lets the fleet occupy more of the sector, so movement itself is the clock. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light)
- Design was driven by "one high-level feeling" rather than fixed mechanics. — [GDC Vault: Designing Without a Pitch – FTL Postmortem](https://gdcvault.com/play/1018034/contactUs)

**Into the Breach (2018, older)**
- Enemies telegraph their moves, "turning each match into a kind of puzzle". Dodging a hit can still damage a building that matters more than the unit (grid/collateral). — [Game Developer, Road to the IGF](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-)
- No fog of war and no hit percentages. Justin Ma: they wanted a game that didn't depend on how smart the AI was, because in typical strategy games a smart AI move usually means a loss for the player. — [PC Gamer preview](https://pcgamer.com/into-the-breach-preview)
- The only randomness besides spawns is "Resist", a small chance for grid buildings to survive. It was added so players still had a reason to press End Turn when facing a certain loss, and the odds were kept low so players wouldn't rely on it. A main challenge was teaching players to accept sacrificing mechs and pilots. — [Cliqist](https://cliqist.com/2018/03/06/into-the-breach-building-a-better-mech-with-subset-games-justin-ma/)
- Matthew Davis's GDC 2019 postmortem covered cut features and "how much RNG should you use?" (content not accessible). — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025772/-Into-the-Breach-Design); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-2019-an-inside-look-at-into-the-breach)

**Frostpunk (2018, older)**
- Two meters: Hope and Discontent. Laws are the main lever, and every law affects one or both. Late Faith/Order paths can remove Hope as a concern entirely. — [Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/?p=220323); [Epic Games Store article](https://store.epicgames.com/news/how-the-everlasting-winter-became-your-fiercest-enemy-in-frostpunk); [Fandom: Frostpunk laws](https://www.fandom.com/articles/frostpunk-laws-surviving-storm)
- Lead designer Jakub Stokalski demonstrated the child-labour law at E3 2017, and it caused backlash among the population. — [Gaming Nexus](https://www.gamingnexus.com/News/39665/E3-2017-Hands-On-with-11-bit-Studios-Frostpunk)
- Design inspiration: "creeping normality", where small laws normalise harsher ones (the snippet doesn't name the speaker). Stokalski frames the game around the survival of "an entire community". — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=154414); [Gamepressure editorial](https://www.gamepressure.com/editorials/fifth-dont-kill-on-moral-dilemmas-in-video-games/a-shard-of-ice-instead-of-a-heart/zf4b9)
- Game Informer: you are never obliged to pass the harsh laws, "but sometimes they are the only way out"; the game forces tough decisions "without strong-arming" players down ideological paths. — [Game Informer review](https://gameinformer.com/games/frostpunk/b/pc/archive/2018/04/23/frostpunk-review-a-frigid-unrelenting-survival-success)

**Hearts of Iron IV (2016, with forum evidence through 2024)**
- Intent (dev diary 2016): the player draws high-level plans and watches for chances to exploit them while the AI fills in the details. The situation changes, so plans must be adapted. — [Paradox dev diary 45](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/hearts-of-iron-iv-45th-development-diary-19th-of-february-2016.909353/)
- Praise: battle plans cut pointless micromanagement, and players personally command the armoured spearhead while infantry follows the plan. — [Matchstick Eyes](https://www.matchstickeyes.com/2016/08/28/a-world-at-arms-thoughts-on-hearts-of-iron-iv)
- Criticism:
  - After a breakthrough, the AI leaves holes in the front.
  - Redrawing a line reshuffles all divisions and costs organisation.
  - The strength-comparison estimates are unreliable.
  - One player said the HQ/brigade setup turned "micromanagement up to 11".
  - Most players use a hybrid of manual armour and planned infantry.
  - [Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/394360/discussions/0/1752358461534945919); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/394360/discussions/0/4298194004191449812)

**Highfleet (2021)**
- Most play happens on a top-down strategic map. The player detects enemies with sensors, radio interception and code-breaking, and intercepts them with missiles and jets. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HighFleet)
- Intel is manual. You mark radar contacts, wait, mark them again and measure their speed. You tune radio dials to intercept messages. Your own radar emissions reveal you to enemy ELINT, so you may switch radar off. — [Prima review](https://primagames.com/reviews/highfleet-review); [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/highfleet-review/); [GamePretty radar guide](https://gamepretty.com/highfleet-guide-radar-aircraft-ship-systems-and-nuclear-warfare/)
- Map rings show tracking range (yellow) and fuel reach (blue). — [MagicGameWorld](https://www.magicgameworld.com/highfleet-map-view/)
- Criticism: PC Gamer says it "breaks down due to opaque systems and lack of difficulty options". RPS headline: "a brilliant strategy game buried under frustrations". The tutorial over-teaches radio intercepts and under-teaches missiles. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/highfleet-review/); [Wikipedia citing RPS, 26 July 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HighFleet); [Ashlander Analysis](https://ashlanderanalysis.substack.com/p/highfleet-the-false-prophet-simulator)

**Terra Invicta (EA 2022, 1.0 later)**
- You act through a small council of operatives, with two actions per councillor per turn. "Every month becoming a triage exercise." — [Wargamer review](https://www.wargamer.com/terra-invicta/review); [inkl/PC review](https://www.inkl.com/news/terra-invicta-review)
- Criticism:
  - The late-game councillor layer is a "fruitless effort" against enemy councillors.
  - The game is "the best concept… executed in the most boring, repetitive and confusing way".
  - It has a learning curve of 30–100+ hours (aggregated, automated summary).
  - [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1176470/discussions/0/3386168507493486589); [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1176470/terra_invicta)
- The developers floated giving councillors autonomy for defensive/support missions or reducing turn frequency again to fix late-game chores. Whether this shipped is unconfirmed. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1176470/discussions/0/4140564026491977463/?ctp=3)
- The game shifts phase from a geopolitical sim to space strategy around 10–30 hours. — [Turn Based Lovers 1.0 review](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/terra-invicta-1-0-impressions/)

### Inferences
- Across the games, there are three types of pressure:
  - Global clocks: Avatar, rebel fleet, Pandemic outbreak track.
  - Local meters that accumulate from neglect: XCOM EU panic, Frostpunk discontent.
  - Fully telegraphed next-turn threats: Into the Breach.
- The best-liked designs combine a global clock with local neglect meters, and make the neglect visible on the map.

## Which threat-pressure systems feel tense but fair?

### Takeaway
Pressure feels fair when the threat is deterministic or readable before you act, and when you can push back against it with a counter-action. Into the Breach's telegraphs, XCOM 2's Avatar reductions and Pandemic's predictable reshuffle all work this way. Tension needs to escalate in spikes rather than linearly. Clocks that can be fully stalled, or that are predictable enough to exploit, lose their tension late in a campaign.

### Cited Findings
- Into the Breach shows every attack, uses no fog and no hit percentages, and keeps randomness minimal so that losses never come from "smart AI". — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/into-the-breach-preview); [Cliqist](https://cliqist.com/2018/03/06/into-the-breach-building-a-better-mech-with-subset-games-justin-ma/)
- A small "Resist" chance keeps hope alive when loss is certain, but its odds were kept low so it doesn't undermine planning. — [Cliqist](https://cliqist.com/2018/03/06/into-the-breach-building-a-better-mech-with-subset-games-justin-ma/)
- Pandemic's epidemic reshuffle makes known cities recur. This makes escalation readable ("hope and fear") and tunable through the number of epidemic cards. — [Nerdist](https://nerdist.com/article/10-years-of-saving-the-world-a-look-at-the-pandemic-family-of-games/)
- Firaxis designed for "peaks and valleys as opposed to this linear curve", and accepts that players "can't mitigate all" Dark Events. — [PCGamesN](https://pcgamesn.com/xcom-2/xcom-2-declassified-firaxis-on-the-risky-shot-theyre-taking-with-a-new-strategy-layer)
- Avatar clock failure mode: the pressure fades after mid-game, and players can stall it for months by raiding facilities. — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/thoughts-xcom-2/); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/268500/discussions/0/1696040635907895337)
- XCOM EU failure mode: panic resolves at a predictable monthly report, so players time satellites to it. — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/200510/discussions/0/611702631229587679)
- Phoenix Point's lack of an aircraft ETA forces guessing, which players read as unfair. — [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/phoenix-point-year-one-review)

### Inferences
- For a Null spread at night, these lessons apply:
  - Show next-night spread arrows, as Into the Breach does.
  - Keep one global clock that rises a step at a time.
  - Make counter-actions (for example destroying a Null node) reduce the clock.
  - Make clock reductions harder to repeat later in the campaign, so it cannot be stalled.
  - Use Pandemic-style recurrence ("this region flares again"), which stays readable without becoming random.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on player tolerance for doom clocks was found.

## How do the best designs make scanning and intel valuable rather than a chore?

### Takeaway
Intel works when the act of gathering it costs the same scarce resource as everything else, and when it is interruptible and contested. XCOM 2's scan-days can be interrupted by attacks, and Highfleet's radar exposes you to enemy ELINT. Intel becomes a chore when it is manual repetition with no decision attached: Highfleet's dial-tuning and triangulation, and Phoenix Point's slow, unresponsive point-of-interest scanning.

### Cited Findings
- In XCOM 2, scanning spends days while time otherwise stands still. Attacks can cut scans short, and only about six scan sites exist at once ("scarcity of opportunity"). Even the monthly supply drop must be scanned. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/xcom-2-review); [XCOM Wiki](https://xcom.fandom.com/wiki/Geoscape_(XCOM_2))
- Intel is a distinct, scarcer currency, spent to contact regions (expanding reach). — [XCOM Wiki: Supplies](https://xcom.fandom.com/wiki/Supplies)
- XCOM EU satellites combine detection, panic relief, funding and Council grading in one placement. — [Prima](https://primagames.com/news/xcom-enemy-unknown-starter-guide)
- Highfleet: active radar reveals you to enemy ELINT, so detection carries risk. — [GamePretty](https://gamepretty.com/highfleet-guide-radar-aircraft-ship-systems-and-nuclear-warfare/); [Prima](https://primagames.com/reviews/highfleet-review)
- Highfleet's manual mark-wait-mark speed estimation and dial tuning are part of its "opaque systems" critique. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/highfleet-review/); [Prima](https://primagames.com/reviews/highfleet-review)
- Phoenix Point: landmark-by-landmark scanning slowly extends reach. Reviewers flagged unresponsive POIs and repeated mission types. — [Dread Central](https://www.dreadcentral.com/reviews/474381/phoenix-point-review-two-steps-forward-one-step-back/); [GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/?p=42747)

### Inferences
- A Commander scan mini-game should resolve quickly and give one of a small number of readable outcomes, such as revealing the next spread, unlocking a call-in, or raising a region's yield.
- Each scan should cost the same scarce night actions as other choices.
- Scanning should be a placement decision (as with XCOM EU satellites) rather than a repeated input skill. Highfleet's dial-tuning is flavourful but works only for a niche audience.

### Gaps
- No developer statement explaining why XCOM 2 required scanning for supply drops was found.

## How do strategic choices visibly pay off in the tactical layer?

### Takeaway
The clearest links run in both directions. Strategy picks which tactical fight happens and with what modifiers, and tactical results move strategic meters right away.

### Cited Findings
- XCOM EU: mission choice determines which country loses panic and what reward (scientists, engineers) arrives. Losing a mission can end a country's membership. — [Prima](https://primagames.com/news/xcom-enemy-unknown-starter-guide); [TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/XCOM-Enemy-Within/walkthrough/2)
- XCOM 2: Dark Events left unblocked change tactical and economic conditions (for example smaller supply drops). Story and facility missions move the Avatar clock. — [XCOM Wiki](https://xcom.fandom.com/wiki/Supplies); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/268500/discussions/0/135507780428639842)
- Into the Breach: protecting grid buildings during a fight protects the campaign-level power grid. Tactical dodging can harm strategic assets. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-)
- HoI4: plans hand tactics to the AI, which only pays off when the AI executes reliably. Unreliable execution pushed players back to micromanagement. — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/394360/discussions/0/1752358461534945919)
- FTL: the choice of jump (fuel, rebel advance) determines which encounter happens next. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light)

### Inferences
- These lessons apply to Commander, Lieutenant and Logistics:
  - Commander's night priorities should appear as visible modifiers on Lieutenant's auto-battles and Logistics' routes the next day (for example "scouted: enemy comp revealed" or "priority: +supply").
  - Day battle outcomes should visibly tick the night map.
  - The HoI4 lesson is that delegated execution must be legible and trustworthy, which matters for a co-op handoff.

### Gaps
- No sourced material was found on Civilization's or Battle Brothers' strategic-to-tactical link.

## What causes analysis paralysis or chores at the strategic layer, and how is it prevented?

### Takeaway
Chores come from several sources:
- Repeated low-stakes actions every turn (Terra Invicta's councillor triage, Highfleet's manual intel).
- Opaque or unreliable feedback (HoI4 estimates, Phoenix Point's missing ETAs).
- Predictable cycles that reward exploitation over decisions (the XCOM EU monthly report).

Prevention techniques include:
- Capping options: three abductions, six scan sites.
- Freezing time until a commitment is made (XCOM 2).
- Making information perfect so that thinking is about solutions, not guessing (Into the Breach).
- Offering automation for low-value actions (as Terra Invicta's developers proposed).

### Cited Findings
- XCOM EU limits choice to "up to three" simultaneous abductions, picking one. — [Prima](https://primagames.com/news/xcom-enemy-unknown-starter-guide)
- XCOM 2 has about six scan sites, and time is frozen until you scan. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/xcom-2-review)
- Terra Invicta: two actions per councillor per month becomes "triage". Late-game opposing councillors make it a "fruitless effort". The developers floated councillor autonomy or fewer turns. — [Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/terra-invicta/review); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1176470/discussions/0/3386168507493486589); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1176470/discussions/0/4140564026491977463/?ctp=3)
- HoI4 battle plans aimed to remove micromanagement, but unreliable AI and estimates pushed players back to it. — [Paradox dev diary](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/hearts-of-iron-iv-45th-development-diary-19th-of-february-2016.909353/); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/394360/discussions/0/4298194004191449812)
- Into the Breach discussed unlimited undo (Designer Notes #70). It ships one reset per battle, but this is not confirmed in the sources fetched. — [Designer Notes](https://www.designer-notes.com/2022/10)
- Phoenix Point's Geoscape doesn't always pause on important events. — [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/phoenix-point-year-one-review)

### Inferences
- For an iPad co-op night phase, these measures would prevent chores and paralysis:
  - Allow a fixed small number of priority assignments per night (2–3).
  - Offer at most about three flagged hotspots.
  - Use auto-pause or turn structure rather than real time.
  - Auto-resolve or delegate routine actions.
  - Keep a predictable "night report" (as XCOM's Council does), but add variance so it can't be timed and exploited.

### Gaps
- No primary-source paralysis data or player-research metrics were found.

## Developer statements found (and not found)

### Takeaway
Primary quotes were obtained from Garth DeAngelis (XCOM 2), Justin Ma (Into the Breach), Matt Leacock (Pandemic) and the HoI4 dev diary. Solomon's Avatar statements and 11 bit's law-design statements were not found in primary form.

### Cited Findings
- DeAngelis: "You're not just standing and waiting for a mission"; "we want there to be spikes of difficulty"; players "can't mitigate all of them". — [PCGamesN](https://pcgamesn.com/xcom-2/xcom-2-declassified-firaxis-on-the-risky-shot-theyre-taking-with-a-new-strategy-layer)

- Leacock: outbreaks and chain reactions are "fascinating to model"; a disease is a "terrifying, unfeeling, and worthy opponent". — [Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2014/10/13/yahtzee-meets-the-hot-zone/)

### Inferences
- A Frostpunk-style law could map to a co-op Commander "edict" that trades a team-wide buff for a visible cost. The "creeping normality" escalation also suggests ordering edicts so that each one unlocks a harsher next one.
