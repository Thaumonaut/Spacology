WORLD LAB PROOF

Small cells.
One coherent landscape.

The default streamed world regenerates nearby 8 m chunks from the seed as you travel. The finite 128 × 128 m catchment or 256 × 256 m region remain available under World footprint. All use 25 cm or 12.5 cm material voxels. In the finite worlds, the western basalt fault exposes ferrite above a river basin. One root is already surveyed; camera zoom does not reveal hidden resources. The larger region extends the same seeded geography beyond the original boundary; pan to explore it.

The density buttons compare the same geography, seed and resource identity. 12.5 cm regenerates finer voxel ground, not just a closer camera. Trees, water, crew and world bounds keep their metre scale. Choose Root site, then + to inspect full detail; wider views use render-only LOD. The 25 cm baseline remains the default. Performance samples reset when density changes.

Crew follow tasks, not the camera: the explorer surveys frontier hexes and the researcher carries samples to the root camp for study. Tap a hex to assign a task. The hex atlas is a summary layer sampled from the voxel ground; Explore, Logistics and Research show the same hexes through each role. Knowledge is in-memory only; travel is straight-line. Their feet follow the same voxel-ground queries used by inspection. Natural terrain is not editable. This is a visual/engineering lab, not yet the persistent Logistics game.

How it stays small

  • Vertical material runs, rather than objects for every cell.
  • Worker-generated greedy surface meshes; internal faces never reach the GPU.
  • View-based chunk requests, bounded CPU/GPU caches and explicit buffer disposal.
  • Render-only LOD; camera zoom never changes the selected voxel ground or deposit IDs.
  • Reusable character parts; no per-frame voxel meshing. Economy caps pixel density and uses coarser render sampling.

Fixed 50° orthographic tilt and the streamed world at 25 cm are prototype defaults. The finite regional view shows only a portion of the larger world at once to respect chunk-cache limits. Geography is still a single river/ridge template with seeded surface variation, not a finished procedural planet. No phone performance claim.

World beneath your feet

The streamed world keeps the camera at the rendering origin while dragging the world underneath it; crew stay at their task sites. Arrows travel; +/− zoom; Root site returns home. Only nearby chunks stay in memory. Seeded ridges and wet corridors are a streaming fixture, not finished river drainage or a planet. Travel ignores obstacles, water and slopes; this is not character navigation.

Location is an 8 m chunk pair plus a small local offset, retained in the URL. No terrain or visited-world history is saved. Numeric guardrail: ±16,777,215 chunks (about ±134,218 km), not literal infinity. The finite worlds use a separate fixed river/ridge template.

What does it cost?

Frame p50/p95/p99 are recent requestAnimationFrame intervals, including stalls, not GPU timestamps. Generation/meshing times run in a worker. GPU bytes are uploaded vertex buffers only; driver/framebuffers and whole-process memory are unknown. Encoded bytes are the worker's bounded voxel cache; transient mesh/scratch arrays are not resident-cache bytes.

Newly exposed chunks show unfilled background while loading. A previous LOD stays visible during a zoom transition until replacement meshes for the visible view are ready. Rendering approximation never replaces exact ground queries.
