BOUNDED WORLD PROOF

Small cells.
One coherent landscape.

A 128 × 128 metre catchment made from quarter-metre material voxels. The western basalt fault exposes ferrite above a river basin. One root is already surveyed; camera zoom does not reveal hidden resources.

The explorer walks the clearing; the worker carries supplies. Their feet follow the same voxel-ground queries used by inspection. Natural terrain is not editable. This is a visual/engineering lab, not yet the persistent Logistics game.

How it stays small

  • Vertical material runs, rather than objects for every cell.
  • Worker-generated greedy surface meshes; internal faces never reach the GPU.
  • View-based chunk requests, bounded CPU/GPU caches and explicit buffer disposal.
  • Quarter-, half- and one-metre render sampling. World data and deposit IDs never change.
  • Reusable character parts; no per-frame voxel meshing. Economy caps pixel density and uses coarser render sampling.

Fixed 50° orthographic tilt and density are prototype defaults. Coarse views are previews, not construction geometry. No planet-scale or phone performance claim.
LIVE INSTRUMENTATION

What does it cost?

Frame p50/p95/p99 are recent requestAnimationFrame intervals, including stalls, not GPU timestamps. Generation/meshing times run in a worker. GPU bytes are uploaded vertex buffers only; driver/framebuffers and whole-process memory are unknown. Encoded bytes are the worker's bounded voxel cache; transient mesh/scratch arrays are not resident-cache bytes.

Missing chunks show as unfilled background while loading. LOD switches can temporarily reload the view. Rendering approximation never replaces exact ground queries.
