How it works

1
Ten cards, three kindsA coloured spine tells you what a card is before you read it — blue for crew, violet for a fitting, amber for salvage. You never have to parse a card to know its type.
2
Nothing is decided until you confirmCards start neutral. Tap once to keep, again to strip. A running tally shows what you are walking away with, in gold and in shards by element. No card is destroyed by a stray tap.
3
The pairing is on the cardEvery crew card says whether it works with someone already aboard. “Quill marks for you” is the buy decision, answered where the decision is made.
4
Strip value stated, in the right element“strip → 6 order”. So a card you do not want is still legible as a specific contribution to a specific crystal.
5
Roster pressure shown, not enforcedThe last card is outlined red and reads “roster full”. You can still keep it — something else has to go — but the constraint is visible at the moment it bites.
6
Fittings are just another cardEquipment does not get its own system or its own screen. It arrives in the same pack, triaged by the same gesture, and says which of your crew it suits.

Why this is better than three cards

✓
The reveal is an eventTen cards landing is worth watching. Three is a transaction.
✓
Scrapping is the expected outcomeKeeping three of ten feels like curation. Stripping two of three felt like waste, which was the original complaint about the Currency Wars shop.
✓
Mixed types carry the varianceA pack with no crew you want still pays in gold and shards, so a bad pull is disappointing rather than ruinous.
!
The riskTen cards is a lot of reading per shop visit. If a visit takes two minutes, a fourteen-leg voyage spends more time shopping than fighting. Worth timing before committing.
