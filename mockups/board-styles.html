Board styles

Your layout, four ways. Same skeleton throughout: the queue down the left with the round and purse at its foot, enemies in ranks up top, the acting unit rising into the middle, and the on-field / off-field tray anchored at the bottom. Each board is 4:3, the shape of an iPad.

A · Survey instrumentthe readout you already have, tightened

Closest to what exists. Numbers are the hero, colour is reserved for state, everything is a thin rule on dark. Reads well at arm’s length and ages well — but it looks like a tool, and a card game wants a little warmth.

B · Card tablecards with real weight

Cards are paper on felt, with shadows and a warm cream face. The most tactile, and drag-and-drop feels obvious because the pieces look liftable. The risk is that a busy fight gets noisy, and light card faces are harsher at night.

C · Star chartengraved, cartographic

Hairline boxes, a faint starfield, cyan for your side. Fits the fiction best — a survey vessel charting worlds — and it is the calmest of the four for playing in bed. Thin strokes need care at small sizes.

What I would pickand why

C, the star chart, with B’s card weight borrowed for the crew tray only. The chart style fits a survey vessel, stays calm at night, and leaves colour free to mean something — which matters when green is a tag, red is an enemy and amber is whoever is acting. Making only the tray cards feel like physical objects tells your hand where the draggable things are without lighting up the whole screen.

The one thing I would take from D is the acting card: solid amber, hard shadow, twice the size of anything else. In your sketch the actor is the same size as everything else, and it is the one card that should never be missable.
