What changed:
- **Rough rock.** Exposed mountain rock now breaks into strata 0.55 to 1.65 m thick that wander with a 2D warp.
  - Strata are cut back into ledges of uneven height and length. Soft layers are recessed and hard layers jut.
  - A 1 to 3 block chip field roughens every tread.
  - At the two finest levels, hard layers overhang the ledge below as lips.
  - Tones follow the strata: dark, pale and warm grey, with grey-green and rust lichen on the treads.
- **Rubble.** Talus (gully-side rock below crags) and gravel scree are now chip-roughened rubble of gravel, stone and boulder material. The regular striped staircase on the (344, -880) scree is gone.
- **Boulders.** Boulders are rounded-angular polyhedra.
  - They are wider than tall, with a flatter top, and sit on the ground with only their foot embedded.
  - They are undercut toward the contact, so a shadow line wraps the base.
  - They come in clusters of 2 to 5 in falling sizes; a small one sometimes rests on a bigger one.
  - Moss or snow caps the top faces only.
  - Tors stand on ridge crests and peaks.
  - Loose stones lie on alpine ground.
  - There are fewer in montane forest and none on snow or ice.
- **Snow.** Snow gathers in hollows, gullies, cirques and cols, measured as depth below the broad landform plus the erosion crease. Peaks, crests, arêtes and spurs are bare rough rock, except for thin patches on gentle ledges. Below the snowline nothing changes apart from slightly lower snow in deep hollows.
