# Knowledge, ladders and marks outlast power

The roguelites that keep players for hundreds of hours do it **without permanent power**. Slay the Spire, Balatro, Into the Breach, Monster Train, Peglin and Risk of Rain 2 carry four things between runs: a pool of content that grows sideways, a short difficulty ladder you opt into, a mastery or collection overlay ("won at the top difficulty with this card"), and the player's own knowledge. Permanent stat growth is the most criticised model in the genre. Rogue Legacy is its standard example, and Josh Bycer calls it "a forced crutch". Developers who used it later added ways to switch it off (Hades' Routine Inspection, Vampire Survivors' toggles, Dead Cells' Aspects) or replaced it with plain assist options (Hades God Mode, Rogue Legacy 2 House Rules). Steam achievement data shows the same shape in every game. **About 45–75% of owners win once, 10–20% reach the middle of the ladder, and only 2–8% clear the top.** So the end game runs out for most players when novelty runs out, long before the ladder does. Narrative across runs (Hades) and hub building (Darkest Dungeon, Loop Hero) carry players through the middle of the game but end or turn into grind. Live auto-battlers (TFT, Battlegrounds, Super Auto Pets) have no progression end game at all. They keep players by breaking the solved meta on a schedule, which an offline premium game cannot copy. For Voyage, the evidence supports the direction the draft proposal already takes, in this priority order: a **Long Census that records correct readings**, a **short skill-only ladder built from charter × condition weight** whose rungs add new conditions rather than bigger numbers, **mastery marks per crewmate and charter**, **visible accuracy and streak stats**, an **offline date-seeded "Census of the Day"**, and an **assist mode with no penalty** in place of the safety net that power grind usually provides.

A note on evidence. The completion percentages are Steam global achievement rates read on 8 October 2026. They are lifetime figures across all owners, including people who never launched the game, so they understate how far *active* players get. The ratings in the comparison table are this report's judgement from the notes, not a measured metric. A ledger of conflicting and unverified figures closes the report.

## Every successful premium model banks options and knowledge, not stats

Meta-progression comes in five forms, and the research ranks them clearly. **Content unlocks** add cards, relics, squads or characters to the pool. **Permanent power** buys bigger numbers. **Difficulty ladders** (Ascension, Heat, Stakes, Covenant, Cruciball, Eclipse) stack modifiers you opt into. **Collection overlays** (codexes, stickers, frames) record what you have done. **Narrative or hub persistence** changes the world between runs. Least contested are knowledge, ladders, and variety unlocks used as onboarding. Narrative is contested but defensible. Vertical stat grind is criticised most.

Slay the Spire unlocks cards, relics and characters by *cumulative score*, win or lose. Thresholds run from 300 to 2000 points ([slaythespire.info](https://slaythespire.info/en/how-to-unlock-characters-cards-and-relics-spoiler-warning/)). Its 20 Ascension levels are tracked **per character**, and each level adds a concrete rule: more Elites, less healing, the Ascender's Bane curse, one fewer potion slot, and a double boss at A20 ([StS Wiki](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Ascension)). Balatro gives the player **no permanent boons at all**. Unlocks add decks, Jokers and vouchers to the pool ([CBR](https://www.cbr.com/losing-balatro-run-satisfying/)), and its 8 Stakes are unlocked per deck. Each Stake changes a rule (no Small Blind money, Eternal, Perishable and Rental Jokers, one fewer discard) and puts a coloured sticker on every Joker used in the win ([Balatro Wiki](https://balatrowiki.org/w/Stakes)). Into the Breach pays **1 coin per achievement**, and each squad comes with three squad-specific achievements. Coins buy squads that are sidegrades, not upgrades ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2022/07/into-the-breach-advanced-edition-update-is-out-now)). Reviewers say working through the achievements teaches you "how to set up certain kinds of combos" and works as "an extended tutorial" ([gamecritics.com](https://gamecritics.com/dan-lipscomb/into-the-breach-review/); [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/into-the-breach-review); the snippet does not say which outlet wrote which quote). Risk of Rain 2 unlocks items and survivors. Its Eclipse ladder pays **nothing except a marker on the character-select screen**: "strictly a challenge mode to test players' skills" ([RoR2 wiki](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Eclipse)). The closest any of these games come to "power" is Monster Train 2's Pyre Hearts, earned through in-run achievements, and clan XP that unlocks cards and a second hero. Reviewers praised that as a "constant sense of progress" ([FullCleared](https://fullcleared.com/reviews/monster-train-2-review/)). Whether Pyre Hearts are pure sidegrades was not verified.

The case against permanent power is old and consistent. Andrew Doull argued in 2009 that "the only guaranteed point at which the player is open to all lessons is at a complete reset of the game state" ([Doull](https://roguelikedeveloper.blogspot.com/2009/07/permadeath.html)). Josh Bycer wrote in 2015 about Rogue Legacy: "you literally can't play far into these titles on your basic setup and the Meta-game becomes a forced crutch". He said meta should be "icing on the cake", as in The Binding of Isaac ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-pitfalls-of-meta-game-design)). Rogue Legacy's Architect took **70% of gold** to replay a layout, and unspent gold was handed over at the gate, which made the game "a bit of a grindfest" ([Niche Gamer](https://nichegamer.com/reviews/rogue-legacy-review-roguelike-platforming-at-its-finest/); [Riptide Lab](https://riptidelab.com/?p=832)). Players make a subtler point too: meta power "blurs self-evaluation". After a loss you cannot tell whether you need more unlocks or more understanding, and you can "continue playing badly and still win eventually" ([Lemmy thread](https://lonestarpiefed.duckdns.org/post/24798)). Similar threads run on ResetEra ([ResetEra](https://www.resetera.com/threads/im-starting-to-feel-that-stat-based-meta-progression-is-starting-to-ruin-roguelites-generally-speaking.1509337/page-2)) and on Steam for Hades II ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1145350/discussions/0/4358999171576511867/?ctp=2)).

The strongest defence comes from Supergiant. Greg Kasavin's team worked to "take the sting out of failure" ([Inlander](https://inlander.com/culture/hades-writer-greg-kasavin-on-how-he-made-video-game-deaths-drive-a-feel-good-story-22725237)). Even Hades lets experts remove the cushion: Routine Inspection disables **up to 12 Mirror talents** in exchange for Heat ([Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/hades-guide-pact-of-punishment-modifiers-heat)). Over 2013–2022 the industry moved toward **separating "make it easier" from "make me grind"**. Cellar Door said many players *wrongly believed* grinding was mandatory in Rogue Legacy, and built the sequel to fix that ([Gameblog.fr, via search summary](https://www.gameblog.fr/jeu-video/ed/news/rogue-legacy-2-metroidvania-difficulte-philosophie-une-interview-devoile-de-nouvelles-infos-85591)). Rogue Legacy 2 cut the Architect tax to 20% ([God is a Geek, via search summary](https://www.godisageek.com/reviews/rogue-legacy-2-review)) and added House Rules: enemy health and damage from 50% to 200%, "with no penalty at all" ([The Workprint](https://www.theworkprint.com/rogue-legacy-2-review-bigger-bolder-better/123)). Hades' God Mode starts at 20% damage resistance and adds 2% per death, up to 80%. Kasavin said it gives players "a bit more time to study those encounters" ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/hades-god-mode-interview)). He also warned that difficulty design is "proprietary to the game. It's not a one size fits all solution" ([Can I Play That?](https://caniplaythat.com/2021/08/11/hades-god-mode-explained-by-supergiant-games)). Dead Cells' Assist Mode keeps achievements on but hides scores from the daily leaderboard ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/dead-cells-breaking-barriers-update-is-live-adds-assist-mode-and-accessibility-options)). Vampire Survivors made PowerUps **refundable and toggleable** ([VS wiki](https://vampire.survivors.wiki/w/PowerUps)). It is still the genre's sharpest compulsion critique: Luca Galante drew on "his previous experience in the gambling industry", including near-miss effects ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/vampire-survivors-how-developers-used-gambling-psychology-to-create-a-bafta-winning-game-203613)).

Two developer redesigns show the same lesson inside a single game. Eremite found that Against the Storm's tree "conflated two concepts: new content and power-ups". It split them: content now arrives automatically with player level, and power sits in a separate Citadel tree ([Eremite](https://eremitegames.com/progression-update/)). Red Hook replaced Darkest Dungeon 2's linear Hope/XP track because it "didn't afford players any real choice". The new Altar of Hope **reset everyone's progress** and gave 20 Candles as compensation ([GameBanshee](https://gamebanshee.com/xyqwk); [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/darkest-dungeon-2-update-introduces-a-new-progression-system-3318046)). Unlocks used as scaffolding are the clean version of this. LocalThunk added an "unlock all" toggle to Balatro that disables achievements, calling it "simply a net positive mechanic" ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2024/03/18/balatro-interview-mobile-port-localthunk-dlc-plans-updates-new-jokers-demo-feedback/), quote via search snippet). He describes stakes, unlocks and challenges as "guides to help players explore the game's design", and his model is solitaire: "play a few times in an hour, then quit" ([GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250228-balatro-vision-solitaire)).

The psychology behind this is self-determination theory and the overjustification effect. In Deci's 1971 study, unpaid puzzle time rose from 248 s to 313 s while subjects were paid, then fell to **198 s, below baseline**, once payment stopped. Hanus's achievement work separates "informational" rewards, which confirm a goal the player already has, from "controlling" ones, which undermine motivation ([Psychology of Games](https://psychologyofgames.com/2016/10/the-overjustification-effect-and-game-achievements)). Raph Koster's "with games, learning is the drug" ([Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/what-is-the-meaning-of-fun/)) and Tom Francis's "information games", where acquiring and using information is the goal ([EGM](https://egmnow.com/the-rise-of-the-information-game/)), complete the argument. Outer Wilds is the purest case: "knowledge is progress", and its ship log keeps that knowledge so the player doesn't have to ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/753640/discussions/0/2518023667588821798)). No peer-reviewed study measuring roguelite meta-progression's effect on retention turned up, so this framework comes from design argument and adjacent psychology, not from direct genre data.

## The ladder is what the long tail plays, and it is shrinking

Steam funnels from these games share one shape. In **Slay the Spire**, 66.4% unlocked Ascension, 14.8% reached A10, 13.0% killed the Heart and **7.3% cleared A20** ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/646570/achievements)). In **Balatro**, 71.6% won a run, 43.7% won on Red Stake or higher, 30.2% on Black, 12.2% on Gold, and **1.6% won Gold Stake on every deck** ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/2379780/achievements)). **Monster Train** goes from 34.1% at C5 to **8.0% at C25** ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/1102190/achievements)). **Peglin** goes from 25.6% at Cruciball 5 to **2.0% at C20** ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/1296610/achievements)). In **Inscryption**, 41.0% reached the story finale but only 18.3% cleared Kaycee's Mod level 1, and **2.7% achieved Skull Storm** ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/1092790/achievements)). Action roguelites match: Dead Cells' "beat Dracula" sits at 7.7% ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/588650/achievements)) and RoR2's False Son Mastery at 4.4% ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/632360/achievements)). The ladder serves the top 5–15% cheaply. The median player stops when the content stops surprising them.

Long-tail players describe the ladder as *the* reason they stay. Steam posters report "well over 1000 hours of playtime where over 80% is on A20". Experienced players say they win only 5–10% of A20 runs ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/6722218992173007839/?ctp=4); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/2521353993639170616/?ctp=4)). David MacIver describes inventing his own goal, win streaks, because "in order to get really good you have to become reliable" ([drmaciver.com](https://drmaciver.com/notebook/2025/03/win-streaking-and-reliability/)). Monster Train 2 made that official: each Covenant rank keeps its **own win streak** ([MT2 wiki](https://monstertrain2.miraheze.org/wiki/Covenant_Ranks)). Not having a ladder is costly. Wildfrost launched with four bell levels, and players compared it unfavourably with StS's "21 difficulty levels" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1811990/discussions/0/3820783808430111599/)). Eight months later Deadpan shipped the Storm Bell update: 13 selectable bells, a point cap that rises, a True Final Boss gate and an "Overcrank" tier ([Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/wildfrost-the-storm-bell-update-announced-version-1-1-0-patch-notes/)).

Three structural trends matter. First, **sequels are cutting their ladders roughly in half**. Slay the Spire 2 (Early Access since 5 March 2026) has 10 Ascension levels instead of 20, ending in Double Boss ([StS Wiki](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Slay_the_Spire_2:Ascension)). Monster Train 2 runs Covenant 0–10 instead of 25 ([MT2 wiki](https://monstertrain2.miraheze.org/wiki/Covenant_Ranks)). No developer explanation was found, so "fewer, more meaningful rungs" is an inference. Second, **tracking the ladder per character or per deck multiplies length but steepens drop-off**. Balatro's 15 decks × 8 stakes turns a 12.2% one-deck Gold clear into 1.6% for all decks. Hades re-pays bounties per weapon per Heat level ([RPG Site](https://rpgsite.net/feature/10287-hades-pact-of-punishment-heat-modifiers-and-how-to-maximize-your-rewards)). RoR2 multiplies 8 Eclipse levels across every survivor. Third, **long ladders built on stat inflation decay into routine at the top**. At Against the Storm's high Prestige levels, players report that viable options "reduced dramatically", the same openings every map, and "P15+ takes a toll". The meta-power tree is effectively budgeted against the top of the ladder ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1336490/discussions/0/3875969774718716509); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1336490/discussions/0/3547176090245096910); quotes aggregated across threads). Into the Breach's Unfair difficulty goes the other way. It creates "more situations where damage is inevitable", so the trade-offs get harder rather than the numbers getting bigger ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2022/07/into-the-breach-advanced-edition-update-is-out-now)).

Ladders also differ in how you climb them. StS, Monster Train and Peglin use **fixed cumulative** steps. Wildfrost's Storm Bells and Inscryption's skulls are **pick-your-modifiers** systems that double as a custom mode. Kaycee's Mod also gives its ladder a **narrative layer**: each level unlocks in-fiction dev logs ([Inscryption fandom, snippet](https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Kaycee's_Mod)). Dead Cells' Boss Cells show the risk of tying content to difficulty: the seventh biome only opens at 5 BC ([Dead Cells wiki](https://deadcells.wiki.gg/wiki/Boss_Stem_Cells)), so most players never see it.

Honkai Star Rail's Currency Wars is the asker's reference point, and its ladder turns out to be the **most transferable** part of HSR's roguelite design. There are nine ranks from Black Iron to Wealth Creator. You climb by winning Standard Gambit runs "at the highest difficulty available to you", and from Investment Master upward there are gated Ascension Matches ([Game8](https://game8.co/games/Honkai-Star-Rail/archives/550753); [Icy Veins, snippet](https://www.icy-veins.com/honkai-star-rail/currency-wars)). That is a single-player Ascension ladder dressed in PvP rank names. Its difficulty steps are mostly flat stat penalties: stronger enemies, less squad HP, a bigger action-value penalty. Owned characters are "mapped to a more optimal build", which narrows the gap from account power but still gates the roster by gacha ownership. **The notes found no documented complaints specific to Currency Wars.** The frustrations on record belong to HSR generally: per-character material grind, power creep that drives sales, content that feels "more like a chore" ([Boss Rush Network](https://bossrush.net/2024/10/the-failure-of-honkai-star-rail/)), and reward caps that reset every two weeks. Divergent Universe's level-60 reward track needs **48 full-clear runs over about 12 weeks**. Its almost-500-entry Gallery of Possibility codex exists mainly to pay out about 1,070 Stellar Jade ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/all-rewards-and-how-to-get-them-in-the-protean-hero-divergent-universe-in-honkai-star-rail/)). Divergent Universe also has a permanent "Inspiration Circuit" power tree ([Tacter](https://tacter.com/honkai-sr/guides/honkai-star-rail-or-divergent-universe-protean-hero-gameplay-guide-585057b2)). So "Currency Wars without its frustrations" means keeping the rank-ladder fantasy and the shop economy, and dropping the timers, caps, ownership gates and power trees around them.

## End games run out in predictable places, and each filler has a cost

**Narrative across runs carries players through the middle of the game, not the end.** Kasavin made death the delivery mechanism for the story. Bosses "keep a tally of who won this time", and the thing that normally persists in roguelikes, "your knowledge of the mechanics", becomes the story ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/roguelikes-and-narrative-design-with-i-hades-i-creative-director-greg-kasavin)). It cost a studio of fewer than 20 people **more than 22,000 voiced lines** ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026975/Breathing-Life-into-Greek-Myth)). The Hades funnel goes from **46.6% at the first escape, to 23.3% at the main quest, to 8.4% at the epilogue**. Hades II's epilogue sits at 9.4% ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/1145360/achievements); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/1145350/achievements)). Narrative roughly halves the drop-off at each stage rather than removing it. Its real value for a power-free game is that **every run, including a loss, produces something**, which is what permanent power otherwise provides.

**Hubs turn into grind when prices outpace runs, or when the currency outlives the shop.** Loop Hero keeps 100% of resources if you retreat at camp, 60% if you retreat elsewhere and 30% on death ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/loop-hero-return-to-camp-how/)). Its later chapters "deteriorate into more of a grind for resources", and players asked for an "unlock all" button ([Goonhammer](https://www.goonhammer.com/loop-hero-a-boardgame-rpg-hybrid-with-an-excellent-gameplay-flow); [RPGFan](https://rpgfan.com/review/loop-hero)). Patch 1.1 answered with speed sliders, not cheaper prices ([TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/news/loop-heros-update-brings-in-expedition-saves-expedition-and-flight-speed)). Darkest Dungeon 1 needed a Radiant mode to cut Hamlet costs ([DD wiki.gg](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Modes)). In DD2, candles kept dropping after the Altar was finished. "Remove candles permanently after fulfilling the altar" was described as "a unanimous sentiment" on the forum in January 2024 ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1940340/discussions/0/4138312860566110288)). Red Hook's lasting answer was not a better tree. It was **Kingdoms** (27 January 2025), a free long-form mode about stewarding inns and heroes, which got an "overwhelmingly positive" response ([GamesPress](https://gamespress.com/Darkest-Dungeon-IIs-Brand-New-Free-Game-Mode-Kingdoms-Red-Hook-Studios); [Darkest Dungeon progress report, via snippet](https://darkestdungeon.com/news/kingdoms-progress-report)). Some players return for a **place and cast to care for**, not an unlock tree. Wildfrost's rebuildable town drew a different complaint at launch: quest-gated unlocks the player could not choose. Some quests forced off-strategy play, and runs that ignored quests felt unrewarded ([Pixel Dive](https://pixeldive.substack.com/p/wildfrost-the-cutest-roguelike-deckbuilder); [Finger Guns](https://fingerguns.net/games/2023/05/02/wildfrost-review-switch-frost-smitten/)).

**Collections work when entries carry meaning and the set is bounded.** People with a high need for control prefer items that *complete* a set over items that merely *expand* it ([EGADE](https://egade.tec.mx/en/egade-ideas/research/collecting-brand-strategy)). The genre's best collection layers are mastery records rather than tallies of things owned: Balatro's Stake stickers (Gold Sticker on all Jokers: **0.9%**), Peglin's per-orb record of the highest Cruciball beaten ([Peglin wiki](https://peglin.fandom.com/wiki/Cruciball)), Wildfrost's Chiseled and Gold card frames, and RoR2's Eclipse icons. HSR's Gallery of Possibility is the counter-example. It is large, but it works as a currency bounty, which is the "controlling" framing that overjustification research warns against. The notes did not document how Hades' codex or Dead Cells' bestiary work, so those comparisons are left out.

**Daily seeds are cheap and habit-forming, but they fade without a distinct identity.** Spelunky introduced the shared-seed, one-attempt Daily ([Spelunky Wiki](https://spelunky.fandom.com/wiki/Daily_Challenge_Mode_(HD))). Slay the Spire's Daily Climb adds modifiers, unlocks everything so scores are comparable, and ranks players on a global board ([Into the Spine](https://intothespine.com/2018/03/05/slay-the-spire-daily-climb/)). One long-time player found it repetitive after "a few weeks" ([GreaterWrong](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/MEimkSwwuRgiPaZaY/review-slay-the-spire)). Dead Cells added dailies late ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/dead-cells/dead-cells-update)), and Wildfrost added Daily Voyages with random Storm Bells. A solo iOS developer reports that a permanent Game Center "Daily Wins" tally made a daily mode competitive "with no server" ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/graze-edge-rush/i-added-a-permanent-daily-wins-tally-to-my-game-and-it-changed-how-people-play), self-reported). No premium developer has published retention figures for a daily mode.

**Live auto-battlers replace progression with scheduled disruption.** TFT now ships about three sets a year, each lasting about four months ([seasontimer.live](https://seasontimer.live/blog/tft-all-sets-dates/); Riot's own rationale for dropping mid-set updates could not be fetched). Its lead says the game nearly died in 2020 ([Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/chef-tft-spiel-kurz-vor-tod-heute-besser-als-je-zuvor/)). Hearthstone Battlegrounds resets ratings and churns dozens of minions each season ([Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2025/04/17/hearthstone-battlegrounds-season-10-start/)). Super Auto Pets rotates a free Weekly pack and lets players build custom packs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Auto_Pets)). Backpack Battles sold **640k copies in its first month** with async PvP and no grind, then shipped about 40 updates in a year. Its tail is long but modest, averaging about 1,576 concurrent players in June 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backpack_Battles); [steambase](https://steambase.io/apps/backpack-battles)). Its main criticism, from IGN, was **thin item variety**. Two cautions apply. Monetisation that touches the shared pool is poison: Battlegrounds' paid 4-hero choice ([esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/hearthstone/hearthstone-battlegrounds-rewards-track-season-pass-is-bg-becoming-pay-to-win-with-runestones/)) and The Bazaar's card-gated battle pass ([CardGamer](https://cardgamer.com/games/digital-card-games/the-bazaar/6-reasons-why-the-bazaars-open-beta-caused-an-uproar)) both drew pay-to-win backlash. And servers make a game mortal: Storybook Brawl became unplayable on 1 May 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storybook_Brawl)). The offline auto-battler space with real meta-progression appears **under-served**. That is an opening, and it also means there are few proven templates.

**Ending development on purpose is legitimate.** Dead Cells stopped after Update 35 to avoid the "more of the same" trap ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/evil-empire-has-officially-ended-support-for-dead-cells)). Rogue Legacy 2's final content update shipped in 2023 ([Nintendo Life](https://nintendolife.com/news/2023/05/rogue-legacy-2s-final-content-update-is-now-live-on-switch-here-are-the-full-patch-notes)). Into the Breach's free Advanced Edition (5 squads, 15 achievements, Unfair, lower squad costs) and Dicey Dungeons' Reunion (six rule-twist episodes, about 5 hours) restarted finite games with **authored variety, not more power** ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2022/07/into-the-breach-advanced-edition-update-is-out-now); [Nintendo Life](https://nintendolife.com/news/2022/06/terry-cavanaghs-dicey-dungeons-rolls-out-huge-new-free-dlc-reunion-soon)). Dicey Dungeons' iOS Metacritic score of **98** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicey_Dungeons)) and the mobile ports of Slay the Spire and Into the Breach suggest the "depth + ladder + sidegrades + short sessions" formula carries to tablets, though neither port has published engagement data.

Power creep, one of the standard complaints, shows up in this evidence almost only in live and gacha titles: HSR's sales-driven creep, Battlegrounds' paid hero choice, The Bazaar's gated cards. In premium games the equivalent risk is the quality of late updates (RoR2's Seekers of the Storm DLC, 34% positive on Steam ([IndieCator](https://indiecator.org/2024/08/31/the-risk-of-rain-2-seekers-of-the-storm-controversy/))) rather than stat inflation.

Cosmetics barely appear in the evidence. Monster Train 2's skins and RoR2's Eclipse markers are the only sourced cases, and the tactics games (ITB, FTL, Darkest Dungeon) offer little cosmetic meta. Nothing here shows cosmetics driving long-term engagement in a premium roguelite.

## Comparison: progression and end game, game by game

Ratings run from 1 (weak) to 5 (exemplary) and are this report's synthesis of the notes. **Progression** asks whether the cross-run layer motivates players without grind or crutch effects. **End game** asks how long and how well the game holds its core after the content is unlocked.

| Game | Primary meta model | Ladder | Where it runs out | Prog. | End game | Key caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Prioritised lessons for Voyage

**1. Make the Long Census the progression, earned by correct reading.** This is the highest-priority recommendation because it does the most to replace power. Each entry should move through tiers: *sighted*, then *read correctly once*, then *read correctly under a complicating condition*. That rewards understanding, not exposure, in the Outer Wilds ship-log pattern. Each entry should give something back: lore, a tactical note, or a sentence of the "now Reva counters twice" kind the proposal already requires for rewards. Keep the set bounded and show the gaps as silhouettes. Frame entries as *informational* ("you understood X"), never as currency. **Once the Census is complete it should stop issuing anything.** DD2's candles show the cost of a currency that outlives its shop. Every voyage should add something, including losses. That gives the "failure is productive" feeling Hades buys with 22,000 lines, at a fraction of the content cost.

**2. Build a short, skill-only ladder from charter × condition weight, and make each rung add a condition rather than a multiplier.** The proposal already notes that this escalation comes "for free". The evidence says to keep it short (sequels went from 20–25 down to 10) and to have each rung **change what must be read** (ITB Unfair's forced trade-offs, StS's rule-based Ascension steps), not inflate numbers (HSR's flat penalties, Against the Storm's P15+). This is the most consequential open decision. One global ladder keeps the funnel shallow. A ladder per charter multiplies playtime but steepens the drop-off: Balatro goes from 12.2% to 1.6%. A middle path fits the evidence: one global weight ladder, plus a per-charter "highest weight cleared" record as a mark, not a gate. Currency Wars-style rank names can sit on top for flavour, without the two-weekly reset.

**3. Add mastery marks per crewmate and per charter.** "Cleared at weight N with Reva" stickers or frames follow the Balatro, Peglin, Wildfrost and Eclipse pattern. They extend the end game indefinitely, add zero power, and turn the always-available crew into a collection of *achievements with* rather than *ownership of*. This is the cheapest end-game extender in the genre.

**4. Show reading-accuracy, streak and best-per-charter stats.** Players at the top of these games invent their own goals when the game makes their play legible: MacIver's streaks, MT2's per-rank streak. In a reading game, "reliability" means accuracy. A record of perfect readings, longest correct-reading streak, and clean voyages per charter costs almost nothing to build.

**5. Ship an offline, date-seeded "Census of the Day" with its own identity.** A deterministic date hash fixes the Brief, the charter offer and one featured modifier, works without a network, and can optionally post to Game Center. Give each day a distinct rule identity so it doesn't go stale within weeks. Show the full Brief so scores are comparable (the StS approach). Don't attach a reward you lose by missing a day, because HSR's capped cycles show where obligation loops lead. A pre-authored, date-rotated "weekly charter" pool can stand in for SAP's Weekly pack and TFT-style rotation without live operations.

**6. Offer an assist mode with no penalty in place of the power cushion.** Rejecting meta power removes the casual player's guaranteed path to the credits. The proven replacement is explicit and not shamed: God Mode's gradual resistance, Rogue Legacy 2's House Rules sliders, or Dead Cells' compromise of keeping achievements and separating leaderboards. For a reading game this could mean a Brief that also annotates which crew answer which conditions. It should be a toggle, never a grind.

**7. If anything unlocks, treat it as a curriculum: advance it on every voyage, let the player choose, and include "unlock all".** The crew being always available already avoids the genre's biggest resentment. Any staged introduction of charters or condition families should follow the Into the Breach and Balatro pattern. It should also avoid Wildfrost's launch mistake: never force off-strategy play to progress, and never leave a run unrewarded for ignoring a quest.

**8. Use narrative across runs sparingly and keep it cheap.** Kaycee's Mod dev logs and StS2's Timeline show that light narrative on top of the ladder works. A Long Census "logbook" voice that remarks on the player's readings, failures included, gets most of Hades' benefit without its content bill. If the team ever wants a long-form mode, DD2's Kingdoms suggests something like a multi-voyage survey campaign with time and attrition budgets (in the spirit of Stygian), not a deeper unlock tree.

**9. Plan renewal as authored variety and let the game end.** The proposal's own "hundredth session" table (charters, then modifiers, then boss answers, then crew) matches what the genre shows: Backpack Battles' main criticism was variety, and ITB AE and Dicey Reunion renewed their games with rule-sets, not power. Shipping a complete game and stopping is respected (Dead Cells, RL2). Leave out cosmetics as an engagement lever unless they come as mastery marks. The evidence gives them no independent long-term pull.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes the question. A premium game with no power grind doesn't lack meta-progression. It is running the model the genre's most durable games already use, and its main risk is losing the casual safety net that power provides, not losing the core. The fixes for that risk are known and cheap: progress on every run, an honest assist mode, and the first win designed for the majority (roughly half of owners) while the ladder serves the 5–15% core. Voyage has an advantage none of these games had. A game *about* reading conditions can make its catalogue of correct readings the progression itself, so the extrinsic layer becomes a record of competence rather than a payment. That closes the gap the overjustification research warns about, as long as the Census never turns into a currency.

The main risk to watch is not grind but **sameness at the top of the ladder**. Against the Storm and Currency Wars show what happens when difficulty means bigger numbers: options narrow until every run opens the same way. The proposal's rule that every charter "closes a door and opens one" is the right guard, and it should apply to every ladder rung as well. If every rung changes what the player must read, the top of the ladder stays a reading problem. If it only changes the numbers, it becomes a solved one.
