# Make commanding feel like leverage, not liability

Commanding is fun when one player's few, readable decisions **visibly land in other players' experience as in-world objects they can see and credit**: a crate dropping on your squad, a spawn point appearing, a target marked on everyone's HUD, a fog bank lifting before a fight. It becomes stressful when four things stack up: real-time overload, responsibility without authority over teammates, sole visible authorship of a loss, and dead time waiting on cooldowns. The asymmetric shooter-hybrids (Natural Selection 2, Battlefield, Squad, Hell Let Loose) show the seat is chronically under-filled. The fixes that actually shipped share one trait. **Making the seat more powerful never filled it. Lowering the cost of an empty or bad seat did**: AI commander bots that a human can replace at any moment, builders who inherit construction powers, and call-ins pushed down to squad leaders. Single-player strategy layers (XCOM, Into the Breach, Pandemic) add the rest of the recipe: one readable escalating pressure, a forced "one of N" choice whose neglect shows on the map, and intel gathered as part of that pressure rather than as a chore. Call-in games (Helldivers 2, StarCraft II co-op, World in Conflict) contribute a three-tier cooldown cadence and two-step, telegraphed targeting. No surveyed game runs a phase-based commander alongside real-time players, so Voyage's plan of night as a war-map phase and day as support call-ins has no direct precedent. It does, however, target the documented pain points squarely. Its main risk is that day call-ins decay into the "just give us artillery" operator role Squad players resent, unless each call-in visibly descends from the night plan. One caveat applies throughout: evidence on fill rates, blame and toxicity is almost entirely forum testimony. No developer published usage telemetry for any title.

## Shooter commanders went unfilled because power never offset the burden

The seat's appeal is real and consistent across titles. It offers **outsized impact routed through other people**. A PopMatters writer recalled that in Natural Selection "a great commander could really set herself apart from others" ([PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/159485-natural-selection-2495844417.html)). Unknown Worlds eventually gave commanders their own skill rating, formally recognising commanding as a distinct skill ([Unknown Worlds](https://unknownworlds.com/en/news/introducing-hiveskill-3-0-per-team-and-commander-hiveskill-thank-you-moultano)). CGMagazine captured the split precisely: "Some players thrive on the power of overseeing so many people's chance at victory, while others prefer the smaller goals of a ground unit," and the role "places heavy pressure on one individual" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/natural-selection-2/)). In the co-located bridge games the reward is social instead. Artemis captains "curse a lot, watch their ship get kicked around... and then laugh with their friends as they hit the reset button" ([Nerdist](https://nerdist.com/article/test-your-starship-command-skills-with-artemis-bridge-simulator/)). Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes' designers said "the fun really lives between players" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/finding-the-fun-in-bomb-defusal-with-i-keep-talking-and-nobody-explodes-i-)). That social fun depends on friends in a room and does not transfer to strangers online.

The seat's core loop is remarkably stable across two decades. The commander watches an overhead map, spends a budget on scans, strikes, supplies and spawns, and issues orders that others may ignore. Battlefield 2 gave the commander satellite scans, artillery, a UAV and supply drops ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/battlefield-2-hands-on-on-foot-and-commander-view/1100-6114811/)). Hell Let Loose runs abilities off three resources ticking at **30 per minute plus 10 per held node**, with cooldowns on top ([HLL Fandom](https://hellletloose.fandom.com/wiki/Commander)). Wiki sources disagree on individual costs; Airhead is listed at 75 or 400, likely because of version drift. Nuclear Dawn's commander researches tech that **unlocks new abilities in other players' class kits** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Dawn)). Savage lets the commander promote officers who carry passive buffs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savage:_The_Battle_for_Newerth)). Barotrauma's captain issues radial-menu orders that can be **pre-emptive and saved for quick reassignment**, and must set an autopilot to leave the nav console ([Barotrauma wiki](https://barotraumagame.com/wiki/Orders)).

The failure story is equally consistent. DICE's own diagnosis of Battlefield 2 was that the seat was "a bit flawed": only one person could play it, "it was always the highest ranking one," and commanders "basically spent the whole match lying on the ground on some obscure corner of the map." Battlefield 3 deleted the seat and pushed artillery and recon down to squad leaders so players could "fill the roles that the Commander had, but then also be an active soldier" ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/70295/dice-explains-why-battlefield-3-has-no-commander)). Squad's forum, which is player testimony, names the "#1 issue" as **being overloaded**. Players report that teams tell the commander to "shut up and just give them artillery," and that the role "does not have the ability to support the SLs and reward them for following orders" ([Squad forums](https://forums.joinsquad.com/discussion/774/commander-role); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/393380/discussions/6/1753520327890191260/)). In Natural Selection 2, players report matches with no commander, or a commander who leaves and is never replaced, and note that many players "join to shoot things" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4920/discussions/0/610573751149960116/)). One Hell Let Loose player wrote the most design-relevant sentence in the corpus: "half the times I've played Commander are the times I only meant to drop my team a supply drop or a tank and I end up being roped into playing the role the whole match" ([Sheepdog's Scroll](https://thesheepdogscroll.substack.com/p/hll-6-leadership-intro-learning-to)). That is **latent demand for short, low-commitment command stints**. Carrier Command 2 shows the opposite failure. With no formal captain and no delegation layer, "seven of them don't really have anything to do" ([Co-Optimus](https://www.co-optimus.com/game/10384/pc/carrier-command-2.html)).

The fixes that shipped all lower the cost of the seat being empty. Natural Selection 2 added commander bots that a human can replace simply by entering the command station. Its Build 303 notes record a bug in which bots were counted in eject and concede votes ([NS wiki](https://naturalselection.fandom.com/wiki/Patches_(Natural_Selection_2)/Build_303); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2016/06/natural-selection-2-adds-a-new-map-and-smarter-bots/)). In Savage 2, **builders gain build ability when no commander is present** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savage_2:_A_Tortured_Soul)). Mutiny and eject votes in Battlefield, Natural Selection 2 and Savage remove bad commanders but amplify blame culture. No source measured how often they were used. Battlefield 4 took the other route of a richer full-time seat. It added commander-versus-commander counterplay and a **squad reward for completing orders**, and DICE stated the aim of ensuring "the Commander is dedicated to supporting the whole team" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/battlefield/battlefield-4-commander-mode-gives-load-powers-player-hot-seat)). Reviewers credited the mode ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/battlefield-4-pc-review/)), yet the seat never returned in later Battlefields, and DICE never said why.

Battlefield 4's iPad Commander App is the one direct tablet precedent, and it is a cautionary one. Early reviews were "overwhelmingly negative" over crashes, connectivity and interface ([Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/2013/11/21/new-game-ea-blurs-the-lines-between-mobile-game-and-companion-app-with-battlefield-4-commander/)). The app could read chat but not type in it. EA ended support in 2015, citing commanders moving back to PC and console ([EA forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/battlefield-franchise-discussion-en/bf4-commander/6480798/replies/6480800)). A companion-style command seat is fragile. Voyage's Commander must stand on its own as a full mode.

## Strategy layers succeed with one readable threat and forced, visible neglect

The best-regarded strategic layers give the commander one escalating pressure that can be read at a glance, and a small forced choice in which the unchosen options visibly suffer. XCOM: Enemy Unknown presents "up to three separate attacks happening simultaneously". The player picks one, and panic rises in the countries ignored ([Prima Games](https://primagames.com/news/xcom-enemy-unknown-starter-guide)). Experts learn to triage on purpose: "if the big money contributors are safe, then I don't worry so much about Africa" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/200510/discussions/0/611702631229587679)). XCOM 2 replaced passive waiting with a mobile base. Producer Garth DeAngelis said "you're not just standing and waiting for a mission", and the designers wanted "spikes of difficulty… peaks and valleys as opposed to this linear curve," accepting that players "can't mitigate all" Dark Events ([PCGamesN](https://pcgamesn.com/xcom-2/xcom-2-declassified-firaxis-on-the-risky-shot-theyre-taking-with-a-new-strategy-layer)). PC Gamer named the mechanism: **"scarcity of opportunity," with roughly six scan sites available at once** ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/xcom-2-review)). Pandemic's Epidemic reshuffle returns already-hit cities to the top of the deck. That produces "escalating moments of hope and fear" which stay readable, and difficulty tunes simply by the number of Epidemic cards ([Nerdist](https://nerdist.com/article/10-years-of-saving-the-world-a-look-at-the-pandemic-family-of-games/)). FTL turns movement itself into the clock, because every jump lets the rebel fleet advance ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light)).

Fairness comes from telegraphing. Into the Breach shows every enemy attack in advance and has no fog and no hit percentages. Justin Ma said the team wanted a game that didn't depend on how smart the AI was, because in most strategy games a smart AI move simply means a player loss ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/into-the-breach-preview)). Its one concession to randomness, a low "Resist" chance for grid buildings, exists so players still press End Turn when facing certain loss ([Cliqist](https://cliqist.com/2018/03/06/into-the-breach-building-a-better-mech-with-subset-games-justin-ma/)). Telegraphing also manages blame. When the cost of a choice was visible before the commit, a bad outcome reads as a fair trade-off.

The failure modes are just as instructive. XCOM 2's Avatar clock "functions adequately for the first 50%" and then becomes controllable, held at 3 to 4 of 12 pips or **stalled "for months" by raiding facilities** ([Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/thoughts-xcom-2/); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/268500/discussions/0/1696040635907895337)). XCOM: Enemy Unknown's monthly Council report is predictable enough that players time satellite launches to it ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/200510/discussions/0/611702631229587679)). Phoenix Point's missing aircraft ETAs force guesswork that players read as unfair ([Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/phoenix-point-year-one-review)). Highfleet's manual mark-wait-mark radar triangulation and radio dial-tuning are atmospheric, but feed PC Gamer's verdict that it "breaks down due to opaque systems" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/highfleet-review/)). Highfleet's one strong intel idea is that **active radar exposes you to enemy ELINT**, so detection carries a real cost ([Prima](https://primagames.com/reviews/highfleet-review)). Terra Invicta's two actions per councillor per month turn into "a triage exercise" ([Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/terra-invicta/review)), and late-game councillor play becomes a "fruitless effort" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1176470/discussions/0/3386168507493486589)). Hearts of Iron IV's battle plans aimed to let the player draw high-level plans while the AI filled in details ([Paradox dev diary](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/hearts-of-iron-iv-45th-development-diary-19th-of-february-2016.909353/)). Unreliable execution and strength estimates pushed players back to micromanagement ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/394360/discussions/0/1752358461534945919)). **Delegation only works when the delegate is legible and trustworthy**, and in Voyage the delegates are autopilots and human co-players. Frostpunk offers a different strategic lever: laws that trade Hope against Discontent. Game Informer noted they force tough decisions "without strong-arming" players ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/frostpunk/b/pc/archive/2018/04/23/frostpunk-review-a-frigid-unrelenting-survival-success)).

Intel stays valuable when gathering it spends the same scarce budget as everything else and can be interrupted or contested. XCOM 2 scanning burns days that alien activity can cut short, and Intel is a scarcer currency than Supplies ([XCOM Wiki](https://xcom.fandom.com/wiki/Supplies)). XCOM: Enemy Unknown's satellites bundle detection, panic relief and funding into one placement decision ([Prima Games](https://primagames.com/news/xcom-enemy-unknown-starter-guide)). Intel becomes a chore when it is repetitive input with no decision attached. That is a direct warning for Voyage's Catdoku sensor placement, Papers-Please intel checking and signal-breach mini-games. Each needs to resolve into a strategic consequence the player chose to pursue, not just a skill score.

## Call-ins work as a three-tier cadence with telegraphed arrival

Support commanding in action games converges on one rhythm. There is a cheap, frequent tool, a few mid-cooldown tools, and one rare "ultimate". Helldivers 2's wiki tables list the **Eagle Airstrike at 15 s, Orbital Precision Strike at 80 s, Resupply at 180 s, Orbital 380mm Barrage at 240 s and Orbital Laser at 300 s**. Eagles use a magazine model with automatic or early rearm, while orbitals start their cooldown on impact ([Helldivers 2 Wiki](https://helldivers.wiki.gg/wiki/Stratagems)). StarCraft II's Karax runs the same ladder. Orbital Strike costs 5 energy with no cooldown, Solar Lance has a 120 s cooldown, and Purifier Beam is **locked for an initial 450 s, then 360 s** between uses ([starcraft2coop.com](https://starcraft2coop.com/commanders/karax)). That initial lockout keeps the ultimate from deciding the opening and builds anticipation. World in Conflict pays for off-map support with Tactical Aid points earned by capturing and killing, so **the supporter's budget scales with the team's success**. It also imposes "a considerable delay on every inbound strike, so timing becomes key" ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/47404/world-in-conflict-multiplayer-preview)). Battlefield 4 tied scans to held capture points and gated cruise missiles and the AC-130 on ground players taking specific objectives ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/battlefield/battlefield-4-commander-mode-gives-load-powers-player-hot-seat)). Deep Rock Galactic charges 80 Nitra from a **shared team pool** for a resupply pod, so ordering one competes with everyone's mining ([DRG Wiki](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/Resupply_Pod)). Company of Heroes 3's Italian campaign is the closest precedent in the notes for Voyage's split. A **turn-based campaign map** feeds real-time battles ([Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/company-heroes-3-review-a-middling-war-effort-despite-ambitious-scope)), and call-ins there depend on supporting forces being nearby on that map ([Prima Games](https://primagames.com/reviews/company-of-heroes-3-review)). The strategic layer decides which call-ins exist where.

How a call-in lands for the recipient matters as much as its numbers. The well-received examples use **two-step targeting**: choose the ability, then place a marker the world can see. Helldivers 2's thrown stratagem ball, Deep Rock's beacon and Battlefield 4's visible strike zone all work this way. They then add a readable delay and a voiced arrival: "Supply pod requisitioned. Launching now" ([DRG Wiki](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/Resupply_Pod)). Karax's community guide advises warning your ally 5 to 10 seconds before Chrono Wave so they have resources ready to spend ([starcraft2coop.com](https://starcraft2coop.com/commanders/karax)). The buff pays off only if the recipient knows it is coming, and Voyage should build that warning into the system. Battlefield 2's satellite scan showed enemies only to the commander, who had to relay them by voice ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/battlefield-2-hands-on-on-foot-and-commander-view/1100-6114811/)). That is the canonical example of help that evaporates. Battlefield 4's High Value Target mark, shown to the whole team for 45 s, is the fix ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/battlefield/battlefield-4-commander-mode-gives-load-powers-player-hot-seat)). Mechabellum, closest to Voyage's Lieutenant auto-battler, puts all control before the fight. Mike Morhaime describes the appeal as being able to "pause to think after each turn" ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/mechabellum-gets-a-second-chance-as-dreamhaven-relaunches-the-mech-autobattler/)). A commander strike landing mid-auto-battle is therefore one of the few live interventions in that mode. It should shift odds rather than decide fights, so the Lieutenant's planning still feels earned.

Helldivers 2's Galactic War is the most relevant cautionary tale about the strategic layer above call-ins. A human Game Master chose "where the enemies attack," while "the war fought and liberation of planets — that's all you" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/helldivers-2-galactic-war-game-master-joel-1851297376)). That clean split between directed pressure and player agency produced legendary stories such as Malevelon Creek. Yet Arrowhead's Mikael Eriksson admitted in 2026 that it was "**never clear if the actions of the community actually lead to different outcomes**," and redesigned around campaigns where "it will always be clear what the active Campaign is, how it's going, what the outcomes have been, and what's at stake" ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/helldivers-2-to-start-galactic-campaigns-this-month-studio-is-working-on-roguelite-planet-warfronts)). That quote comes via secondary coverage, since the original blog post was not read. PC Gamer adds that crowds coordinate poorly across several targets ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/third-person-shooter/arrowheads-latest-major-order-is-exploiting-the-helldiver-2-communitys-greatest-weakness-communication)). In a small co-op group, choosing among targets becomes the commander's core decision rather than a coordination failure.

## Blame, bossiness, idle time and overload each have known structural fixes

**Blame** follows sole visible authorship of a loss. Natural Selection 2 ties defeat to the commander's command stations, and reviewers advise that "the best way to learn how to play commander is by not playing commander" ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/natural-selection-2/); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/natural-selection-2-review/1900-6399998/)). Several structural answers recur. Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes allows **two strikes before detonation** ([Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/?p=185268)). Its hidden pacing system adds disruption when players are doing well and avoids feeding a losing team, because "we don't want to accelerate a death spiral" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/finding-the-fun-in-bomb-defusal-with-i-keep-talking-and-nobody-explodes-i-)). Into the Breach's telegraphs turn losses into readable trade-offs. Self-determination theory research supports paying the commander in **competence and relatedness** rather than win or loss alone: in-game competence and autonomy independently predict enjoyment, and relatedness does so in social play ([Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf)). No study measures commander blame directly. This part of the case rests on reviews and forum threads.

**The alpha-player problem** has a mature board-game toolkit. It includes hidden or role-locked information, simultaneous play, time pressure and information kept in each player's own zone ([Moontower Meta](https://moontowermeta.com/the-alpha-player-problem/); [Don't Eat the Meeples](https://www.donteatthemeeples.com/p/mitigating-quarterbacking-cooperative-games)). The latter's author concludes that quarterbacking is "rarely a phenomenon of the game's design and more of the game's players." Keep Talking gives the strongest model for both directions of the problem. The defuser sees the bomb but not the manual, and the experts see the manual but not the bomb. The designers "needed to have a reason for the players to talk to each other" ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023113/Designing-Asymmetric-Gameplay-For-Keep)). Commanders get ignored when they hold only authority. They get heeded when they hold **information or capabilities others need**. Battlefield 4's "follow the order, earn a squad asset" loop pays followers for cooperating ([Prima](https://primagames.com/eguides/battlefield-4-eguide/battlefield-bootcamp/extended-play)). A claim that Pandemic designer Matt Leacock wants mechanisms where "everyone can shine" reached the notes only through a blog summary ([lestmyopinions](https://lestmyopinions.com/2018/07/29/the-alpha-gamer-problem-and-how-to-solve-it/)).

**Idle time** appears in two forms. The first is waiting on cooldowns, with Squad players complaining of long rearm intervals and Battlefield 2 commanders lying prone all match. The second is waiting on the slowest player in a shared clock. Civilization's lineage shows the standard toolkit. Civ III scaled the clock with empire size and ended turns early when all humans finished ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/civilization-iii-play-the-world-updated-preview/1100-2894699/)). Civ VI shows who is still deciding and lists "random players who are slow" as the main delay ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/289070/discussions/0/340412734660313957)). Civ V's simultaneous turns created a race condition in which "players that click first get to act first" ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/civilization_v/b/pc/archive/2010/09/10/multiplayer-hands-on)). Frozen Synapse and Mechabellum remove waiting altogether: everyone plans at once, presses PRIME, and watches the resolution together ([Bit-tech](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/pc/frozen-synapse-interview/1/)).

**Overload** is a presentation problem as much as a load problem. Zach Gage's "Three Reads" principle orders the screen by prominence. The first read is immediate hooks, the second is important but non-obvious state, and the third is fine detail for players who dig in. Into the Breach is the model and Slay the Spire's icon soup the counter-example ([Gigazine summary of GDC 2018](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20180501-game-design-subway-legibility)). Bad North's developers built a slow-motion, "slightly turn-based" feel and audio you could follow with your eyes closed ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2018/05/feature_battling_the_viking_horde_with_bad_north_dev_plausible_concept)).

The audience question cuts against a deep-strategy seat. Quantic Foundry has no "leadership" motive. Its **Strategy motive has fallen from the 50th to the 33rd percentile of its 2015 baseline across about 1.7 million respondents**. This figure comes via secondary coverage, because the primary page returned 403, and Quantic Foundry claimed no cause ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Strategy-a-dying-breed-in-gaming-study-claims-interest-wanes-in-tactical-gameplay-mechanics-despite-steady-player-counts.840658.0.html)). A 2024 survey of 3,220 League of Legends players linked a preference for Support and Jungle roles to coalition-building, cognitive empathy and leadership, though only as correlations ([Primenjena psihologija](https://primenjena.psihologija.ff.uns.ac.rs/index.php/pp/article/view/2535)). Siitonen's study of online strategy leadership found players describing "leadership as work" ([Inderscience](https://www.inderscience.com/offers.php?id=41484)); only the abstract was available. **No source gives the share of players who want to lead versus support.** The asker's uncertainty about what commander players want is the field's uncertainty too. The most defensible reading is that the seat attracts two motives. Strategists want prediction proven right. Enablers want to have helped a friend at the key moment.

## Prioritised recommendations for the Commander spine

The leaning design is sound. Night as war-map planning matches what works in XCOM and Pandemic and removes real-time overload. Day as support call-ins matches Helldivers 2, Battlefield 4 and Karax and gives the commander something to do while others play. The recommendations below aim to make each half pull its weight and keep the seat cheap to leave empty.

| Priority | Recommendation | Why (evidence) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Make every day call-in descend from the night plan.** A scanned front unlocks a strike there, a prioritised route makes its supply drop cheaper, and a revealed Null node enables the escort. | Prevents the "artillery operator" feel Squad players resent; mirrors XCOM's strategy-picks-the-fight link | Inference from forum anecdote plus XCOM design |

The evidence is thinnest exactly where the asker's uncertainty sits. No source measures what share of players want to command, how often the seat goes unfilled, or how recipients feel about the help they receive. The Strategy-decline figure and several developer quotes come through secondary press. Hell Let Loose costs and Battlefield 4 mutiny thresholds are disputed or unconfirmed. Unverified items are excluded: a 30-second Battlefield 2 scan recharge, a Jake Solomon quote on the Avatar Project from a fan site, and Into the Breach's per-battle reset. The fastest way to close the gap is a playtest split: have some testers play only the night map (with call-ins on autopilot) and others only the day call-ins (with the night on autopilot), then compare which they choose to replay.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the Commander problem. The question is less how to make commanding powerful enough to want than how to make it **cheap to pick up, cheap to put down, and impossible to miss when it helps**. Shooter commanders failed as permanent jobs with veto power over a team's fun. Strategy layers succeeded as small, legible choices made against a readable threat. Voyage's structure lets it take both lessons at once. The phase split removes real-time overload, the autopilots remove the cost of an empty seat, and the shared globe gives every commander action a place to land in a teammate's own mode.

That structure also opens up the commander audience the asker worries about. If the night and day halves can be played independently, the seat serves two players. The strategist reads the Null's next move. The enabler, the asker's own taste, drops a crate on a friend's starving front and sees their callsign on it the next morning. Designing for that enabler first is defensible, because the strongest positive evidence in this research concerns felt, credited help, not strategic depth. Strategy as a motive is the one in measurable decline.
